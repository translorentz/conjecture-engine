\part{Cut-rank laws for graph states, forests, and random width}

\medskip\noindent\emph{Added to the deposit: 30 August 2026.}\par\medskip

For a finite simple graph $G=(V,E)$ and $S\subseteq V$, write
\[
 \rho_G(S)=\rank_{\mathbb F_2}A_G[S,V\setminus S],\qquad
 Z_G(x,y)=\sum_{S\subseteq V}x^{|S|}y^{\rho_G(S)}.
\]
For an edge-labelled graph over $\mathbb F_q$, the same symbol denotes rank
over $\mathbb F_q$; label zero means that the edge is absent.  Fixing a
nontrivial additive character $\chi\colon\mathbb F_q\to\mathbb C^\times$, its
associated $q$-level graph state is
\[
 |G\rangle=q^{-|V|/2}\sum_{z\in\mathbb F_q^V}
 \chi\!\left(\sum_{u<v}A_{uv}z_uz_v\right)|z\rangle .
\]
This explicit convention applies to every prime power, including even
characteristic.  We write $\operatorname{rw}$
and $\operatorname{bw}$ for rank-width and branch-width, $\nu$ for maximum
matching number, and $\operatorname{Odd}_G(A)$ for the vertices having an odd
number of neighbours in $A$.

The fifteen conjectures below form four connected programmes: quenched and
extreme cut-rank laws for dense random graph states; shape, stability,
extremal, and reconstruction laws for forests; extensive width constants in
random regular, sparse, and percolated graphs; and typical rigidity and orbit
entropy under local complementation.  The graph-state Schmidt-rank identity,
local-complementation invariance, the Oum--Seymour theory of rank-width, and
the known order-of-magnitude bounds for random graphs are calibrations, not
new claims~\cite{HeinEisertBriegel04,Oum05,OumSeymour06,LeeLeeOum12}.

\begin{proposition}[Cut-rank calibrations]\label{as:basic}
\textup{(i)} Across the bipartition $S\mid V\setminus S$, the graph state
$|G\rangle$ has Schmidt rank $q^{\rho_G(S)}$ and entropy
$\rho_G(S)\log q$.  Binary local complementation, and its weighted
finite-field analogue, preserve the whole labelled function
$S\mapsto\rho_G(S)$.  \textup{(ii)} If $F$ is a forest and a
uniform random bipartition is chosen, its crossing-edge set is a uniform
subset $B\subseteq E(F)$, every $B$ has the same colouring-fibre size, and
\[
 \rho_F(S)=\nu((V(F),B)).
\]
\textup{(iii)} Put $P_{q,0}=1$,
$P_{q,k}=\prod_{i=1}^k(1-q^{-i})$, and
$P_{q,\infty}=\prod_{i\ge1}(1-q^{-i})$.  The limiting nullity law of a
uniform $m\times(m+s)$ matrix over $\mathbb F_q$, for fixed $s\ge0$, is
\[
 \pi_{q,s}(d)=q^{-d(d+s)}
 \frac{P_{q,\infty}}{P_{q,d}P_{q,d+s}},\qquad d\ge0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \textup{(i)}, put $M=A_G[S,V\setminus S]$.  Diagonal unitaries remove the
phases contributed by edges internal to either side, leaving the coefficient
matrix
\[
 q^{-|V|/2}\bigl(\chi(u^{\mathsf T}Mv)\bigr)_
 {u\in\mathbb F_q^S,\ v\in\mathbb F_q^{V\setminus S}}.
\]
Its rows are additive characters indexed by the image of $M^{\mathsf T}$:
there are exactly $q^{\rank M}$ distinct rows, they are mutually orthogonal,
and they occur with equal multiplicity.  Hence the nonzero Schmidt
coefficients are equal and number $q^{\rank M}$, which gives the asserted
rank and entropy.  For $q=2$ this is the standard graph-state identity
~\cite{HeinEisertBriegel04}.  Define weighted local complementation at $v$
with parameter $a\in\mathbb F_q$ by
$A'_{jk}=A_{jk}+aA_{jv}A_{vk}$ for $j\ne k$, with zero diagonal, and define
vertex scaling at $v$ by multiplying all incident labels by
$b\in\mathbb F_q^\times$.  These are the finite-field graph operations of
Bahramgiri--Beigi for arbitrary prime-power $q$
~\cite{BahramgiriBeigiEnumerating07}; in their odd-prime graph-state setting
they describe the local Clifford action
~\cite{BahramgiriBeigiNonbinary06}.  Across a fixed cut, weighted local
complementation adds multiples of the $v$-row to the other crossing rows
when $v\in S$, or of the $v$-column to the other crossing columns when
$v\notin S$; vertex scaling multiplies one crossing row or column by a
nonzero scalar.  This direct argument works over every finite field, so every
cut rank is preserved over $\mathbb F_q$.  On a forest, fixing one vertex
colour in each connected component makes the map from the remaining vertex
two-colourings to crossing-edge sets bijective; hence every crossing-edge set
has exactly $2^{\kappa(F)}$ colouring preimages.  A bipartite forest's
adjacency bi-matrix has rank over
$\mathbb F_2$ equal to its term rank: a nonzero maximal minor has a unique
perfect matching, since two perfect matchings would create a cycle.  This is
\textup{(ii)}.  Finally, the exact rank count for finite rectangular matrices,
divided by $q^{m(m+s)}$ and then sent to infinity, gives \textup{(iii)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[Quenched balanced-cut nullity universality]\label{as1}
Fix a prime power $q$ and $s\in\{0,1\}$.  Let $G_{2m+s,q}$ be the loopless
complete edge-labelled graph in which every unordered vertex pair receives
an independent uniform label in $\mathbb F_q$ (with zero interpreted as no
edge), and set
\[
 \mu_G(d)=\binom{2m+s}{m}^{-1}
 \#\{S:|S|=m,\ m-\rho_G(S)=d\}.
\]
Then $\mu_G$ converges in probability, in total variation, to
$\pi_{q,s}$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
Every fixed cut matrix is uniform.  Its exact finite nullity probability is
\[
 q^{-d(d+s)}
 \frac{P_{q,m}P_{q,m+s}}
 {P_{q,d}P_{q,d+s}P_{q,m-d}},
\]
which tends to Proposition~\ref{as:basic}(iii); the content is self-averaging
over the exponentially many, heavily overlapping cuts of one state.  A proof
would turn the random-matrix nullity law into a quenched entanglement spectrum
and requires dependence-sensitive concentration, not independent sampling.
The nearest results concern fixed-cut stabilizer entanglement and average cut
rank, rather than the empirical law of all cuts of one realization
~\cite{NguyenOum20}.

\begin{conjecture}[Dependence-sensitive extreme balanced-cut defect]\label{as2}
In the setting of Conjecture~\ref{as1}, let
$D_{\max}(G)=\max_{|S|=m}(m-\rho_G(S))$.  Then
\[
 \frac{D_{\max}(G_{2m+s,q})}
 {\sqrt{(2m+s)\log_q2}}\ \longrightarrow\ 1
\]
in probability.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
The exact rank formula gives
$\Pr\{\operatorname{nullity}\ge d\}=q^{-d(d+s)+O_q(1)}$ uniformly for
$d=O(\sqrt m)$, while
$\log_q\binom{2m+s}{m}=(2m+s)\log_q2+O(\log m)$; these formulas force the
displayed first-moment location.  The union-bound upper estimate follows.
The lower bound is a genuine de-clustering problem: overlapping cuts share
quadratically many entries, complementary cuts are identical when $s=0$,
and rare defective cuts might occur in large dependency clusters.  Binary
enumeration at orders $10$--$18$ gave
mean normalized values $0.72$--$0.80$, increasing across that range; this
calibrates the scale but is not treated as independence evidence.

\begin{conjecture}[Poisson law for random graph-state distance]\label{as3}
For $G\sim G(n,1/2)$ put
\[
 f(G)=\min_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V}
 \bigl(|A|+|\operatorname{Odd}_G(A)\setminus A|\bigr)
\]
and
\[
 \Lambda_n(\ell)=\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}\binom na
 \Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n-a,1/2)\le \ell-a\}.
\]
Uniformly along integer sequences for which $\Lambda_n(\ell)$ remains in a
compact subset of $(0,\infty)$, the number of nonempty $A$ whose displayed
weight is at most $\ell$ converges in total variation to
$\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda_n(\ell))$.  Consequently
$\Pr(f(G)>\ell)=e^{-\Lambda_n(\ell)}+o(1)$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
Here $f(G)=\delta_{\rm loc}(G)+1$, the minimum degree up to local
complementation plus one.  To make the code equivalence explicit, choose
$\omega\in\mathbb F_4\setminus\mathbb F_2$ and set
\[
 C_G=\{x(A_G+\omega I):x\in\mathbb F_2^V\}\subseteq\mathbb F_4^V.
\]
Symmetry of $A_G$ makes the rows trace-Hermitian orthogonal, and their
$\omega$-diagonal entries make them independent over $\mathbb F_2$, so
$C_G$ is self-dual.  The word indexed by $A=\operatorname{supp}x$ has
support $A\cup\operatorname{Odd}_G(A)$.  Consequently its minimum nonzero
Hamming weight is
\[
 \min_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V}|A\cup\operatorname{Odd}_G(A)|
 =\delta_{\rm loc}(G)+1=f(G),
\]
where the equality to $\delta_{\rm loc}(G)+1$ is the standard
local-minimum-degree characterization
~\cite{JavelleMhallaPerdrix12,CattaneoPerdrix15}.  The mean has the
equivalent exact form
\[
 \Lambda_n(\ell)=2^{-n}\sum_{w=1}^{\ell}\binom nw(3^w-1),
\]
whose first-order threshold is already known.  The new assertion is the
Poisson finite-length law: the sharp random-linear-code analogue does not
cover the symmetric graph-state generator, where $A$, $B$, and
$A\triangle B$ create structured dependencies~\cite{HaoEtAl22}.

\begin{conjecture}[Typical rigidity of the labelled cut-rank function]\label{as4}
For $G\sim G(n,1/2)$, with probability tending to one, every graph $H$ on
the same labelled vertex set satisfying
\[
 \rho_H(S)=\rho_G(S)\qquad(S\subseteq V)
\]
is obtainable from $G$ by local complementations, without permuting the
vertices.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
The converse is standard, but universal completeness is false.  A current
unlabelled-orbit census finds no collision below nine vertices and respectively
$1,3,15$ colliding LC-orbit pairs at orders $9,10,11$
~\cite{BurchardtDeJongVandre24}; structural descriptions through minimal local
sets give another nearby boundary~\cite{ClaudetPerdrix25}.  Those results are
unlabelled, whereas the conjecture and the exact verifier concern one fixed
labelled vertex set.  Exhaustive enumeration through six vertices found exact
equality between labelled cut-rank fibres and labelled local-complementation
orbits, including all $760$ fibres at order six.  The conjecture says that the
alternative isotropic representations behind structured exceptions have
vanishing density.  Since local-unitary equivalence preserves every cut rank,
the claim would also imply generic LU--LC; no redundant LU--LC conjecture is
stated separately.

\begin{conjecture}[Positive-rank-tail log-concavity]\label{as5}
For every connected graph $G$, let
$c_r(G)=\#\{S\subseteq V(G):\rho_G(S)=r\}$ and
$R=\max_S\rho_G(S)$.  Then
\[
 c_r(G)^2\ge c_{r-1}(G)c_{r+1}(G),\qquad 2\le r<R.
\]
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
The statement deliberately begins at rank two, and that starting point is
sharp.  The connected graph on vertices $0,\ldots,12$ with edge set
\[
\begin{split}
\{&(0,8),(0,10),(1,2),(1,8),(2,7),(3,7),(3,12),(4,6),\\
  &(4,7),(4,8),(5,6),(5,8),(7,8),(7,9),(7,10),(7,11),\\
  &(7,12),(8,9),(8,11),(8,12)\}
\end{split}
\]
has exact cut-rank profile
$(2,28,410,1896,3296,2208,352)$, so
$c_1^2=784<820=c_0c_2$.  Thus rank-one log-concavity is false, not merely
unproved.  Conditioning on $|S|$ fails already at order four.  The positive
tail passed all $1{,}252$ nonempty graph-atlas
graphs on at most seven vertices and a separate seeded stress test on $8{,}887$
connected random graphs of orders $8$--$13$.  A proof would expose a
previously unknown negative-dependence or injection principle for graph-state
entanglement multiplicities.

\begin{conjecture}[Full log-concavity for percolated forests]\label{as6}
For a forest $F$, define
\[
 a_j(F)=\#\{B\subseteq E(F):\nu((V(F),B))=j\}.
\]
Then $(a_0(F),\ldots,a_{\nu(F)}(F))$ is log-concave.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
By Proposition~\ref{as:basic}(ii), these coefficients are, up to a constant
colouring fibre, the cut-entanglement law of a forest graph state.  The rooted
recursion
\[
 A_T=\prod_i(A_i+2B_i),\qquad
 B_T=z\!\left(2^k\prod_i(A_i+B_i)-\prod_i(A_i+2B_i)\right),\qquad
 H_T=A_T+B_T,
\]
with $A_{\rm leaf}=1$ and $B_{\rm leaf}=0$, computes the polynomial without
enumerating edge sets.  A fresh independent audit used it to check exact
log-concavity on every unlabelled tree through order $20$; the checked-in
default verifier deliberately runs a smaller reproducible range.  The
boundary is real: real-rootedness
fails at seven vertices for $(1,13,30,20)$, while ultra-log-concavity fails for
the $13$-vertex graph6 tree \texttt{LqD?I?@O??g??@} with profile
$(1,75,432,1060,1360,912,256)$.

\begin{conjecture}[Hurwitz stability of the percolated-matching polynomial]\label{as7}
For every forest $F$, every zero of
\[
 H_F(z)=\sum_{B\subseteq E(F)}z^{\nu((V(F),B))}
\]
has strictly negative real part.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
$H_F$ is neither the classical matching polynomial nor the maximal-matching
polynomial: its coefficients count all edge subsets according to the maximum
matching number they induce.  The preceding two-state recursion gives a
forest-specific mechanism, distinct from the Hochster-strand Hurwitz
conjecture in Part~VII.  A fresh independent audit certified the Routh
conditions exactly on every unlabelled tree through order $20$, on paths
through order $80$, and on several large structured families; the checked-in
default verifier uses exact coefficients with a smaller numerical root scan.
There can be no uniform root-modulus margin, since
$H_{S_n}(z)=1+(2^{n-1}-1)z$ has a negative root tending to zero.

\begin{conjecture}[Path--star stochastic extremality under bond noise]\label{as8}
If $T_p$ retains each edge of an $n$-vertex tree independently with
probability $p\in(0,1)$, then for every integer $k$,
\[
 \Pr\{\nu((S_n)_p)\ge k\}\le
 \Pr\{\nu(T_p)\ge k\}\le
 \Pr\{\nu((P_n)_p)\ge k\}.
\]
For $n\ge4$, equality at every threshold characterizes the star and path,
respectively, up to isomorphism.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
The star lower bound is elementary; the substantive assertion is the
stochastic path upper bound and its equality rigidity.  Classical results
make the path and star extremal for the ordinary Hosoya index
~\cite{HuangShiXu18}, but no theorem located in the audit gives the all-threshold
law for the maximum matching of an edge-percolated tree.  Independent exact
Bernstein-basis comparison through order $17$ checked every probability $p$
simultaneously; the default verifier runs through order $11$.  A proof should
couple the rooted matching recursions or identify tree transformations
preserving stochastic order.

\begin{conjecture}[Fixed-leaf broom minimum]\label{as9}
For $2\le L\le n-1$, obtain $B_{n,L}$ from $K_{1,L}$ by subdividing one
pendant edge $n-L-1$ times.  For every fixed $p\in(0,1)$, among all
$n$-vertex trees $T$ with exactly $L$ leaves,
\[
 \nu((B_{n,L})_p)\ \le_{\rm st}\ \nu(T_p)
\]
and equality in distribution holds if and only if $T\cong B_{n,L}$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
The endpoint classes $L=2$ and $L=n-1$ are unique; the claim begins at
$3\le L\le n-2$.  The same broom is already known to minimize the ordinary
Hosoya index at fixed leaf count~\cite{PanXuYangZhou07,YuLv07}; the new content
is stochastic domination of the maximum matching after bond percolation.
Independent coefficientwise checks through order $17$ found neither an
inequality failure nor another equality case.

\begin{conjecture}[Typical tree reconstruction from the entanglement enumerator]\label{as10}
Let $T_n$ be a uniform labelled tree on $[n]$.  With probability tending to
one, every tree $T'$ satisfying $Z_{T'}(x,y)=Z_{T_n}(x,y)$ is isomorphic to
$T_n$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
Universal reconstruction is false and is not asserted.  The first collision
occurs at order $15$: the nonisomorphic graph6 trees
\texttt{NpCa?C@?a??@?@O???G} and
\texttt{NpCc?D??G?\_@O???g??} have the same $Z$.  Exhaustive enumeration
found this to be the only collision class among all $7{,}741$ unlabelled
$15$-vertex trees, with none through order $14$.  The claim asks whether this
exceptional sparsity persists asymptotically; an independent re-audit found no
further unrooted collision through order $18$.  The decisive obstruction is a
Schwenk-type pair of nonisomorphic rooted limbs with identical rooted transfer
data for $Z$~\cite{Schwenk73}: such limbs could be substituted in arbitrary
hosts, and fringe-subtree laws would then threaten the typical
claim~\cite{Janson16}.  The same re-audit
found no rooted transfer collision through order $16$.  A proof must rule out
substitutable limbs or show that their occurrence is negligible.  The
consequence is typical identifiability, not an algorithmic inversion claim.

\begin{conjecture}[Rank-width density of random regular graph states]\label{as11}
For each fixed $d\ge3$, if $G_{n,d}$ is a uniform simple $d$-regular graph
and $n$ tends through values with $dn$ even, there is a constant
$\kappa_d\in(0,1/3]$ such that
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{rw}(G_{n,d})}{n}\longrightarrow\kappa_d
\]
in probability.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
Expansion gives linear lower bounds and the universal decomposition bound
gives the upper endpoint, but neither yields convergence.  The proposed
mechanism is a limit for the minimum balanced cut-rank profile of the
configuration model, plus recursive balanced decompositions losing only
$o(n)$.  Exact dynamic programming on small cubic graphs gave mean widths
$2.45,2.60,2.90,3.00$ at orders $8,10,12,14$ in an exploratory scan; the
checked-in verifier uses a smaller seeded sample and reports its own values.
Recent average-case complexity results for random regular graph states do not
establish a normalized rank-width limit~\cite{GhoshHangleiterHelsen25}.

\begin{conjecture}[Cubic branch-width density and kernel transfer]\label{as12}
As $N\to\infty$ through even integers, there is a constant $\beta_3>0$ such
that for a uniform simple cubic graph $H_N$,
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{bw}(H_N)}N\longrightarrow\beta_3
\]
in probability.  In addition, let $M_N$ be a cubic configuration multigraph
on $N$ vertices and, conditional on its realization, let $H_N^*$ be any
simple graph obtained using $r_N=o(N)$ edge deletions and insertions.  Form
$S_N$ by subdividing all but $b_N=o(N)$ edges of $H_N^*$ at least once,
then applying arbitrary further subdivisions and taking arbitrary one-vertex
sums with trees.  Uniformly over these choices,
\[
 \operatorname{rw}(S_N)=\operatorname{bw}(H_N^*)+o(N).
\]
Consequently, if $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, $\varepsilon^3n\to\infty$, and
$G_n\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon)/n)$, then
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{rw}(G_n)}{\varepsilon^3n}
 \longrightarrow \frac43\beta_3
\]
in probability.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
Oum's incidence-graph theorem gives rank-width within one of branch-width
after subdividing every edge once~\cite{Oum08}; the explicit transfer clause
is the missing robustness statement for unreplaced edges, repairs, repeated
subdivision, and pendant trees.  The factor $4/3$ is the cubic-kernel vertex
density.  Existing weakly supercritical results determine only the
$\Theta(\varepsilon^3n)$ scale~\cite{DoErdeKang24}.

\begin{conjecture}[Rank-width surface tension for off-critical percolated grids]\label{as13}
For fixed $p\in[0,1]\setminus\{1/2\}$, let $Q_L(p)$ be bond percolation on
the $L\times L$ square grid.  There is a deterministic $\tau_{\rm rw}(p)$
such that
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{rw}(Q_L(p))}{L}\longrightarrow\tau_{\rm rw}(p)
\]
in probability, with $\tau_{\rm rw}(p)=0$ for $p<1/2$,
$\tau_{\rm rw}(p)>0$ for $p>1/2$, and $\tau_{\rm rw}(1)=1$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
Below criticality all components are logarithmic.  Above it, percolated-grid
tree-width is linear~\cite{LiMuller17}; to deduce a positive rank-width
coefficient one still needs an $\Omega(L)$ balanced crossing-edge bound, or a
precise bounded-degree width comparison, after which the bounded row/column
weight rank inequality supplies linear cut rank.  At $p=1$, Jel\'inek's exact
formula $\operatorname{rw}(Q_L)=L-1$ fixes the normalization
~\cite{Jelinek10}.  The new content is existence of a surface-tension
constant, plausibly through almost-subadditive rectangle gluing; the critical
point is deliberately left open.  No grid experiment is claimed by the
checked-in verifier.

\begin{conjecture}[Saturation of local-complementation orbit entropy]\label{as14}
Let $\mathcal O_{\rm LC}(G)$ be the set of labelled graphs obtainable from
$G$ by local complementations.  For $G\sim G(n,1/2)$,
\[
 \frac1n\log_2|\mathcal O_{\rm LC}(G)|\longrightarrow\log_2 3
\]
in probability.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
Bahramgiri and Beigi express the binary orbit size as
$\varepsilon(L)/\lambda(L)$, where $\varepsilon(L)$ counts Eulerian vectors
of the isotropic system and $\lambda(L)$ is its index, yielding the universal
bound $|\mathcal O_{\rm LC}(G)|\le3^n$
~\cite{BahramgiriBeigiEnumerating07}.  The conjecture therefore asserts two
separate mechanisms: $\varepsilon(L)=3^{n-o(n)}$ and
$\lambda(L)=2^{o(n)}$.  Seeded exact enumeration
gave mean normalized logarithms $1.285,1.320,1.376,1.383$ at orders
$7,8,9,10$, with sample maxima reaching $1.471$, versus
$\log_2 3=1.585$.  Existing orbit maps reach small orders
~\cite{AdcockEtAl20}; a proof would establish maximal
labelled-representative entropy, not by itself an algorithmic lower bound.

\begin{conjecture}[Rank-width density curve for sparse Erd\H os--R\'enyi graph states]\label{as15}
For every fixed $c>0$ there is a deterministic $\kappa_{\rm ER}(c)$ such that
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{rw}(G(n,c/n))}{n}\longrightarrow
 \kappa_{\rm ER}(c)
\]
in probability, where $\kappa_{\rm ER}(c)=0$ for $c\le1$ and
$\kappa_{\rm ER}(c)>0$ for $c>1$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Significance.}
The qualitative phases are known: subcritical components have bounded
rank-width, critical components are sublinear, and every fixed supercritical
density has linear rank-width~\cite{LeeLeeOum12,DoErdeKang24}.  The new claim
is existence of the normalized expectation limit; standard bounded-difference
concentration is then expected to give convergence in probability.  Its proposed
mechanism passes to the two-core and kernel, proves a limit for balanced
cut-rank optimization under the local weak degree law, and shows that
attached trees do not alter the leading width.  Conjecture~\ref{as12}
predicts the boundary asymptotic as $c\downarrow1$ but does not imply the
fixed-$c$ limit.  The checked-in verifier makes no direct sparse
Erd\H os--R\'enyi width claim.
