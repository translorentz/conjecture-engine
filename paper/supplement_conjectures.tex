\begin{abstract}
This manuscript gives exactly twenty-five candidate conjectures in five programmes:
connected prime fields, arithmetic first-arrival fields and class groups, finite
logarithms and algebraic tori, arboreal arithmetic dynamics, and adelic factorization
processes.  Every numbered statement is intended to be independently research-worthy:
it introduces a canonical operator, process, invariant, classification, or phase
boundary rather than merely recording a familiar Gaussian, Poisson, Gumbel,
Bateman--Horn, or Poisson--Dirichlet consequence.  Each entry states its mechanism,
nearest literature boundary, first decisive theorem, and a failure mode that would reveal
missing mathematics.  The collection is screened against the cited literature but does
not claim professionally certified priority or truth.
\end{abstract}

\section{Top-tier admission standard}

A statement is counted among the twenty-five only when it passes all eight gates below.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{T\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.8em]
\item \textbf{Canonical object.} The ensemble, normalization, topology, and order of limits are fixed before data are inspected.
\item \textbf{Structural mechanism.} The claim identifies an arithmetic, geometric, spectral, or adelic source rather than merely a limiting distribution.
\item \textbf{Independent content.} It introduces an invariant or transformation not already contained in another numbered statement.
\item \textbf{Discrimination.} At least two serious models make different predictions.
\item \textbf{Leverage.} Truth would organize a family of problems, not one statistic.
\item \textbf{Counterfactual value.} Falsity would expose a missing invariant, obstruction, or scale.
\item \textbf{Proof interface.} A smaller theorem is visible and mathematically natural.
\item \textbf{Novelty boundary.} The nearest known theorem or conjectural framework is named and the additional content is stated explicitly.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Evidence convention.}
The companion script is deterministic and deliberately modest.  It verifies exact
identities, searches for elementary counterexamples, compares local models, and reports
finite-range tensions.  No computation is offered as proof or priority certification.

\section{Programme I: connected prime-pattern fields}

Let $H\subset\Z$ be finite and admissible, and write
\[
 \Lambda_H(n)=\prod_{h\in H}\Lambda(n+h),\qquad
 Y_H(x,L)=\sum_n w\!\left(\frac{n-x}{L}\right)
 \bigl(\Lambda_H(n)-\SG(H)\bigr),
\]
where $w$ is a fixed smooth compactly supported function and $\SG(H)$ is the
Hardy--Littlewood singular series
$\prod_p(1-\nu_H(p)/p)(1-1/p)^{-|H|}$, with $\nu_H(p)$ the number of residue
classes occupied by $H$ modulo $p$.  The window position $x$ is
sampled from $[X,2X]$ with density $dx/(x\log2)$ and
$X^\eps\le L\le X^{1-\eps}$.  For translated motifs $H_i+t_i$, their \emph{exact
overlap type} records every equality among shifted prime constraints.  The connected
singular series $\SG^{\mathrm c}(K_1,\ldots,K_r)$ is obtained by M\"obius inversion over
set partitions of the indexed tuple $(K_1,\ldots,K_r)$.

\begin{conjecture}[Connected motif generating functional]\label{c1}
Fix admissible motifs $H_1,\ldots,H_m$ and put
\[
 \mathcal Z_{X,L}(\mathbf z)=
 \E_x\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^m z_iY_{H_i}(x,L)\right).
\]
For every fixed total degree $R$, the degree-$\le R$ Taylor polynomial of
$\log\mathcal Z_{X,L}$ has a uniform asymptotic expansion indexed by connected exact-
overlap diagrams of at most $R$ translated motifs.  A diagram whose coincidence hypergraph connects the $r$ translated sets
$H_{i_1}+t_1,\ldots,H_{i_r}+t_r$ contributes, at the leading order of its stratum,
\[
 W_\tau(L;\mathbf t)\,
 \SG\Bigl(\bigcup_{j=1}^r(H_{i_j}+t_j)\Bigr)
 \prod_v(\log X)^{m_v-1},
\]
where $W_\tau(L;\mathbf t)=L\int_{\R}\prod_{j=1}^rw(u+t_j/L)\,du$ is the
archimedean window-overlap weight of the diagram and
$m_v$ is the multiplicity of the prime constraint at $v$: any set partition that
separates a coincidence loses at least one factor of $\log X$, so the M\"obius
alternation leaves the single-block moment as the leading term of a connected diagram.
The same alternation cancels the leading terms of disconnected diagrams, whose
contribution is carried by the connected singular series of their disjoint blocks at
strictly smaller logarithmic order, reducing for the fully disjoint type to
$\SG^{\mathrm c}(H_{i_1}+t_1,\ldots,H_{i_r}+t_r)$ with no logarithmic enhancement.
The log powers are therefore partition dependent and are never factored across the
alternation, and every prime-power counterterm is included in the local weight attached
to its multiplicity.  Ordering
terms lexicographically by powers of $L$ and $\log X$, the remainder after any fixed
truncation is smaller than the last retained scale, uniformly in the mesoscopic range.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This is a multitype connected field theory for prime constellations.  It simultaneously
organizes covariance, odd moments, mixed motifs, overlap singularities, and lower-order
prime-power effects.  Montgomery--Soundararajan and Kuperberg provide the closest
one-field moment frameworks, while constrained singular-series sums provide the nearest
local input \cite{MS,Kuperberg,KRR}.  The new content is the complete connected
multitype functional.  The first decisive theorem is the full third-cumulant formula for
two distinct pair motifs.

\begin{conjecture}[Complete overlap-renormalization filtration]\label{c2}
Fix a finite motif family $H_1,\ldots,H_m$.  Apply the degree-two specialization of
\cref{c1} and order all of its connected covariance strata by their asymptotic scales
$s_1\succ s_2\succ\cdots$ (lexicographically in powers of $L$, $\log X$, and the
renormalized disjoint scales).  In the mesoscopic range scales sharing the same total
power of $L$ and of logarithms differ only by powers of $\theta=\log L/\log X$ and
merge into a single stratum whose limiting form is polynomial in $\theta$.  The
filtration, the graded convergence, and the eigenvalue asymptotics below are asserted
for every fixed $\theta\in(\eps,1-\eps)$, with each $A_\nu=A_\nu(\theta)$
nondegenerate for every such $\theta$.  Recursively subtract the earlier strata and let $A_\nu$
be the limiting covariance form at scale $s_\nu$, and put
\[
 \mathcal W_\nu=\bigcap_{\mu<\nu}\ker A_\mu.
\]
For every fixed truncation of this ordered diagram expansion, the spectral projections
of the covariance matrix converge to the associated graded spaces
$\mathcal W_\nu/\mathcal W_{\nu+1}$, and every eigenvalue whose first nonzero term occurs
within the truncation is asymptotic to $s_\nu$ times a positive eigenvalue of the induced
form $A_\nu$.  Vectors annihilating all retained forms pass canonically to the next
connected diagram scales of \cref{c1}.

Consequently every asymptotic variance scale of a finite motif statistic is generated by
a connected overlap or renormalized disjoint diagram from \cref{c1}, and no scale
external to that diagrammatic expansion occurs.  The filtration is invariant under replacing the
motif family by one with isomorphic exact-overlap incidence algebra and identical local
Euler weights.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
The claim classifies all mechanisms by which a finite linear combination of prime motifs
can become rigid.  It predicts a canonical renormalization filtration, not merely one
variance formula, and gives an algorithm for constructing observables that expose deeper
arithmetic strata.  A counterexample would identify a genuinely new source of rigidity
outside overlap and disjoint correlation.  The first theorem is a three-motif example in
which one overlap form has a rational kernel and the next form is nondegenerate on it.

\begin{conjecture}[Mesoscopic local--spectral trace formula]\label{c3}
Fix motifs $H_i,H_j$ and a Schwartz function $\phi$ with $\phi(0)=0$.  Let
$Q^{\mathrm{loc}}_{ij}(\phi;L)$ be the exact-overlap-renormalized singular-series form
\[
 \sum_{t\in\Z}
 \left[L\int_{\R}w(u)w(u+t/L)\,du\right]
 \phi(t/L)\,\SG^{\mathrm c}(H_i,H_j+t).
\]
Independently, let $\Lambda_T^\sharp$ be the symmetrically truncated Weil
explicit-formula distribution: its pairing with every compactly supported smooth test
function is the standard pole term minus the sum over nontrivial zeros with
$|\Im\rho|\le T$, together with the trivial-zero and archimedean terms, while the omitted
prime-power contribution is retained as its named remainder.  Define
$Q^{\mathrm{spec}}_{ij}(\phi;X,L;T)$ by replacing every von Mangoldt factor in the
smoothed motif covariance by $\Lambda_T^\sharp$, expanding the product, and applying the
same exact-overlap M\"obius projection as on the local side.  Thus the two forms are
defined independently, with pole, trivial-zero, diagonal, and prime-power terms
carried explicitly.

The translation sum is finite, since the compact support of $w$ makes the window
overlap vanish for $|t|\gg L$.  For every sufficiently large fixed $A$, take
$T=(X/L)(\log X)^A$.  After adding the explicit tail of the spectral truncation,
\[
 Q^{\mathrm{spec}}_{ij}(\phi;X,L;T)
 -Q^{\mathrm{loc}}_{ij}(\phi;L)
 =o\bigl(V_{ij}(X,L)\bigr)
\]
uniformly for $X^\eps\le L\le X^{1-\eps}$ and for $\phi$ in bounded Schwartz sets,
where $V_{ij}$ is the first nonzero disjoint covariance scale.  The identity holds
jointly for every fixed matrix of motifs and test functions and is unchanged if $A$ is
increased.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This is a genuine trace formula: the Euler-product and multiple-zero sides are defined
independently and compared only after common smoothing and named counterterms.  It extends
the Goldston--Montgomery and Chan prime/zero equivalence from one prime-counting field to
products of shifted von Mangoldt functions \cite{GM,Chan}.  Failure would reveal a
mesoscopic covariance functional missing from both Hardy--Littlewood and known spectral
correlation languages.  The first theorem is the pair-motif covariance with one factor
unshifted.

\begin{conjecture}[Arithmetic polymer Gibbs law for prime motifs]\label{c4}
Fix an admissible motif $H$.  A finite set $A\subset\Z$ of occurrence starts is
connected when the graph on $A$ with an edge between $u$ and $v$ whenever
$(H+u)\cap(H+v)\ne\emptyset$ is connected, and a polymer is a finite connected set
$A$ such that $U_A=\bigcup_{t\in A}(H+t)$ is admissible.  In the truncated
multiple-zero expansion used in \cref{c3}, let $Z_{X,L,R}$ be the vector of all zero
monomials contributing to the one-point motif intensity whose total spectral height is
at most $RX/L$, and let $\lambda_{X,L,R}(t)$ be the one-point intensity functional it
determines at the start $t$.  The pair of $Z_{X,L,R}$ and the occurrence process
converges jointly in law as $X\to\infty$, and all conditioning below is on the
limiting mode vector, a nondegenerate random object, not on a sigma-field generated at
finite $X$, where conditioning on deterministic functions of the window position would
degenerate to the sample point.

Write $e(A)=|A|\,|H|-|U_A|$ for the number of shared prime constraints of a
polymer, so $e(A)\ge|A|-1$ for connected $A$ by the multiplicity rule of \cref{c1}.
For every fixed polymer $A$, under the limiting joint law,
\[
 \frac{\rho^{(|A|)}(A\mid Z)}
      {(\log X)^{e(A)}\prod_{t\in A}\lambda(t)}
 \longrightarrow
 \frac{\SG(U_A)}{\SG(H)^{|A|}}
\]
in probability as $R\to\infty$, uniformly for bounded polymer diameter.  The polymer
activities therefore carry the factors $(\log X)^{-e(A)}$, every non-singleton
activity vanishes in the limit, and the cluster structure is a finite-$X$
triangular-array phenomenon rather than a nondegenerate limiting decoration.  The
conjectured law is the first-order extremal-index expansion: the cluster-start
fraction of the occurrence process obeys
\[
 \theta_X(H)=1-\frac{c(H)}{\log X}+o\!\left(\frac1{\log X}\right),
 \qquad
 c(H)=\sum_{t\ne0}\frac{\SG\bigl(H\cup(H+t)\bigr)}{\SG(H)},
\]
the sum running over the shifts sharing exactly one prime constraint, with the
higher-order polymer corrections carrying higher powers of $1/\log X$ through the
analogous connected activities.  The finite-$X$ Gibbs polymer expansion built from
these activities is projectively consistent, its cluster partition function is
subcritical for every fixed admissible $H$, and the rescaled occurrence process is,
to first order, a Cox process of Poisson starts directed by the low-zero one-point
intensity with the polymer corrections entering at the scale $1/\log X$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
The conditional normalization is what makes the law canonical: the
low-zero field controls only the varying one-point intensity, while complete union
singular series control the residual cluster geometry, at the explicit
$(\log X)^{-e(A)}$ activity scale that the multiplicity rule forces.  The law uses
polymers of every size: for $H=\{0,6\}$, starts $0,6,12$ already form a legitimate
three-occurrence polymer, and for that motif
$c(H)=2\,\SG(\{0,6,12\})/\SG(\{0,6\})$.  A proof would connect
Hardy--Littlewood constants, explicit-formula fields, Palm theory, and extreme gaps
in one object.

\begin{conjecture}[Topological expansion of non-Gaussian prime cumulants]\label{c5}
For every connected exact-overlap diagram in \cref{c1}, form the bipartite incidence
complex whose vertices are motif occurrences and distinct surviving prime constraints,
with an edge for incidence.  Let $g$ be its first Betti number after forced coincidences
are contracted.  Once all larger coincidence strata have been subtracted, a diagram of
Betti number $g$ is smaller than a tree diagram with the same external motifs by at
least $(\log L)^{-g}$.  The complete leading coefficient at each logarithmic order is
the sum of the connected diagram weights with the corresponding Betti number.  In
particular, the first nonzero odd cumulant of a balanced motif statistic, a
combination $Y_c$ whose coefficient vector annihilates every coincidence-stratum form
of \cref{c2}, is carried by
incidence trees.  For the single-prime field the fully spread connected part
$\kappa^{\mathrm{sp}}_{2m+1}$, defined by subtracting every coincidence stratum as in
\cref{c2}, obeys
\[
 \kappa^{\mathrm{sp}}_{2m+1}=(c_m+o(1))L^m(\log L)^{m+1},
\]
where $c_m$ is the total connected tree weight.  The raw cumulant adds the explicit
coincidence diagrams of \cref{c1}, which carry powers of $\log X$ and are computable
term by term, so the tree law is a statement about the spread diagrams, exactly the
regime of Kuperberg's singular-series sums.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This predicts a topological genus expansion for prime statistics: every independent
cycle costs a logarithmic order.  Kuperberg's odd-moment conjecture is the one-field
calibration, and the incidence-homology filtration with its motif-dependent
coefficients is the new mechanism \cite{Kuperberg}.  A failure would show that logarithmic suppression
is controlled by an arithmetic invariant not visible in diagram topology.

\section{Programme II: arithmetic first-arrival fields and class groups}

For a prime modulus $q$ and $a\in\Fq_q^\times$, let $p(q,a)$ be the least prime in the
class $a$ and put
\[
 T_q(a)=\frac{\Li(p(q,a))}{q-1},\qquad
 \mathcal N_{q,a}=\sum_{p\equiv a\, (q)}
 \delta_{\Li(p)/(q-1)}.
\]
The probability space chooses $q$ uniformly among primes in $[Q,2Q]$ and then chooses
$a$ uniformly from $\Fq_q^\times$.  Write $e(x)=\e^{2\pi ix}$.

\begin{conjecture}[Connected first-arrival functional]\label{c6}
Let $w^{\mathrm c}_{r,Q}$ be the connected factorial-cumulant measure of the random point
process $\mathcal N_{q,a}$.  For every fixed $r\ge2$ there is a locally finite signed
measure $K_r$ on $\R_+^r$ such that
\[
 (\log Q)^{r-1}w^{\mathrm c}_{r,Q}\Longrightarrow K_r
\]
vaguely, and $K_1$ denotes the limiting intensity measure, which in this clock is
Lebesgue measure on $\R_+$.  The measure $K_r$ is obtained by averaging, over the
modulus aspect, the connected Hardy--Littlewood densities of $r$ primes constrained to
one residue class.  For every nonnegative compactly supported $f$ in a fixed uniform ball on which the
Laplace functional is analytic,
\[
 \log\E\exp(-\langle f,\mathcal N_{q,a}\rangle)
 =\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{(-1)^r}{r!}
 \int\prod_{j=1}^r(1-e^{-f(u_j)})\,dw^{\mathrm c}_{r,Q}(\mathbf u),
\]
and the asymptotic expansion obtained by inserting the limits $K_r$ is uniform to every
fixed connected order.  In particular the entire survival function, not merely its
first correction, is determined by the hierarchy $(K_r)_{r\ge2}$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This is an arithmetic point-process theory of least primes.  The coupon-collector model
of Li--Pratt--Shakan supplies the nearest extremal analogy, but not a connected Laplace
functional \cite{LPS}.  Truth would determine waiting times, occupancy covariance,
cover times, and terminal clustering from one hierarchy.  The first theorem is the
existence and explicit evaluation of $K_2$ on compact time intervals.

\begin{conjecture}[Gauss-polyspectral reciprocity for the least-prime field]\label{c7}
Set $T_q(0)=\Li(q)/(q-1)$ and centre the resulting field $f_q$ on all of $\Fq_q$.  Let
\[
 A_q(\xi)=q^{-1/2}\sum_{a\bmod q}f_q(a)e(-a\xi/q),\qquad
 M_q(\chi)=(q-1)^{-1/2}\sum_{a\ne0}f_q(a)\overline{\chi(a)}.
\]
On the subspace of fields vanishing at $0$ with zero mean the Gauss transform is an
explicit unitary matrix, and the recorded value at $0$ enters through an explicit
rank-one correction, and together these give an explicit Gauss matrix $U_q$,
invertible between the centred subspaces on which the two transforms are faithful,
with
$\mathbf M_q=U_q\mathbf A_q$, and the tensor identity below needs only linearity.  Hence, for every $r\ge2$, the full cumulant tensors obey
the exact finite identity
\[
 \operatorname{Cum}_r(\mathbf M_q)
   =U_q^{\otimes r}\operatorname{Cum}_r(\mathbf A_q).
\]
The usual additive constraint $\sum\xi_j=0$ and multiplicative constraint
$\prod\chi_j=1$ are contractions of these full tensors.  The constrained slices alone
are not asserted to determine one another.

After dyadic averaging and normalization by the first nonzero connected scale, the full
additive cumulant tensors converge to the Fourier transforms of the connected arrival
kernels $K_r$ of \cref{c6} carried on the zero-sum frequency sublattice, that is to
$\delta_{\sum_j\xi_j\equiv0}\otimes\widehat K_r$.  Applying the exact Gauss tensors before passing to the limit
gives the same limiting contractions as the $r$-fold explicit-formula correlations of
Dirichlet $L$-function zeros on the multiplicative side.  Thus the local arrival kernels
and character-zero correlations are two exactly compatible tomographic representations
of one non-Gaussian field.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
The $r=1$ Gauss identity is elementary.  The conjecture is the complete higher-order
reciprocity between arithmetic first-arrival cumulants and character spectra.  It
supplies a finite-field analogue of a trace formula and makes every mixed higher moment
computable from either side.  A failure would identify information destroyed by all
additive and multiplicative polyspectra despite their exact tensor duality.

\begin{conjecture}[Rubinstein--Sarnak decorated terminal law]\label{c8}
Assume GRH and a $q$-aspect linear-independence/random-phase hypothesis for the low zeros
of primitive Dirichlet $L$-functions.  Let $x_q$ satisfy
$\Li(x_q)/(q-1)=\log(q-1)$ and, for $A>0$, define the centred low-zero field
\[
 G_{q,A}(a)=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{q,A}}\Re
 \sum_{\chi\ne\chi_0}\overline{\chi(a)}
 \sum_{\substack{L(1/2+i\gamma,\chi)=0\\|\gamma|\le(\log q)^A}}
 \frac{x_q^{i\gamma}}{1/2+i\gamma},
\]
where $\sigma_{q,A}^2$ is the average over reduced classes of its variance under
the random-phase law, the same convention as in the compensator below.  Under the
random-phase law, its finite-dimensional distributions converge, first as a random
prime modulus $q\in[Q,2Q]$ tends to infinity and then as $A\to\infty$, to the
Rubinstein--Sarnak Gaussian field at terminal scale.

Let $b_Q$ be determined by
$\E_q\#\{a:T_q(a)>b_Q\}=1$ and define the random directing measure
\[
 \Lambda_{q,A}(dg\,dx)=
 \frac{\theta_{\mathrm{occ}}e^{-x}}{q-1}
 \sum_{a\in\Fq_q^\times}
 e^{G_{q,A}(a)-\frac12\Var(G_{q,A}(a))}
 \delta_{G_{q,A}(a)}(dg)\,dx.
\]
Here $\theta_{\mathrm{occ}}=\E^{\mathrm{Palm}}\bigl[|\mathcal A|^{-1}\bigr]$,
with $\mathcal A$ the terminal polymer cluster, is the cluster-start fraction of the
terminal polymer law, the analogue of $\theta(H)$ in \cref{c4}.  The pair of
$G_{q,A}$ and the point process
$\sum_a\delta_{(G_{q,A}(a),T_q(a)-b_Q)}$ converges jointly in law along the same
successive limits, the directing measures $\Lambda_{q,A}$ converging, under the
random-phase hypothesis, to a nondegenerate log-Gaussian directing measure
$\Lambda_G$.  Conditionally on the
limiting field, the cluster-start process is Poisson directed by $\Lambda_G$, and each
start carries, at finite modulus, the terminal analogue of the polymer corrections
of \cref{c4}, entering at the scale $1/\log q$.  No
conditioning on a sigma-field generated at finite $q$ is invoked, since the field is a
deterministic function of the sample modulus and such conditioning would degenerate.  Thus the cover time, final
class profiles, and their prime-race marks are projections of one decorated Cox process.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
Rubinstein--Sarnak theory describes prime-race distributions for fixed moduli
\cite{RubinsteinSarnak}.  This conjecture places the terminal least-prime problem in a
$q$-aspect Gaussian environment and predicts the complete decorated extreme process,
not a scalar Gumbel shift.  It sharply distinguishes iid occupancy, scalar mixing, and a
field-valued spectral environment.

\begin{conjecture}[Nonlinear spectral response calculus for arithmetic first arrivals]\label{c9}
Let a sequence of modulus ensembles have a small explicit-formula perturbation of the
arrival intensity
\[
 V_Q(a,t)=\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal C_Q}v_{Q,\chi}(t)\chi(a),
 \qquad \|V_Q\|_{T}\to0,
\]
where $\mathcal C_Q$ has uniformly bounded cardinality and, on every fixed interval
$[0,T]$, the canonical norm is
$\|V\|_T=\sup_{a,0\le t\le T}|V(a,t)|$.  Assume that the connected hierarchy of
\cref{c6} satisfies an exponential cluster bound there, so its Laplace functional is
analytic in a fixed $\|\cdot\|_T$-ball.  If $S_Q(a,t)$ and
$S_Q^{(0)}(t)$ are the perturbed and ordinary survival functions, then for every fixed
$R$
\[
 \log\frac{S_Q(a,t)}{S_Q^{(0)}(t)}
 =\sum_{m=1}^{R}\mathcal R_m[V_Q^{\otimes m}](a,t)
   +o(\|V_Q\|_{T}^{R})
\]
uniformly on compact $t$-ranges.  The causal Volterra operator $\mathcal R_m$ is the
universal linked-cluster contraction of the full hierarchy $(K_r)_{r\ge1}$ with $m$
marked perturbation insertions, and truncating the connected hierarchy at order $J$
gives a convergent approximation as $J\to\infty$.

If the input character support is $\mathcal C_Q$, the $m$th response is supported exactly
on products of at most $m$ input characters (subject only to coincidences and the trivial
character).  In particular the linear response preserves character rank, while the first
new harmonics occur at quadratic order with coefficients determined by connected
three-point arrival correlations.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This is a nonlinear transfer calculus from zeros to first-hit statistics.  A Siegel zero,
a cluster of low zeros, and an ordinary prime-race bias become different inputs to the
same response operators.  The exceptional-zero rank-one statement is a special case,
while the second-order harmonics provide a direct falsification test.  The first theorem
is the Fr\'echet derivative $\mathcal R_1$ from the two-point occupancy kernel.

\begin{conjecture}[Local-information capacity of odd class groups]\label{c10}
Fix an odd prime $\ell$.  Sample negative fundamental discriminants
$D\in[-2Y,-Y]$, conditioned on a fixed coarse two-primary invariant.  For a set of
odd primes $S_Y$, put $M_Y=\prod_{p\in S_Y}p$, let $\Sigma_Y(D)=((D/p))_{p\in S_Y}$ be the
attainable sign vector, and let $H_Y$ be the logarithm of the number of attainable cells.
Let $\delta_Y(\sigma)$ be the exact CRT density of the sign cell after imposing
quadratic reciprocity and the fixed two-primary data, and let $\mathcal D_Y$ be the
ambient discriminant family.  Write
\[
 \Delta_Y(S_Y)=\sup_\sigma
 \left|\frac{\#\{D\in\mathcal D_Y:\Sigma_Y(D)=\sigma\}}
 {|\mathcal D_Y|\,\delta_Y(\sigma)}-1\right|
\]
over attainable cells with $|\mathcal D_Y|\delta_Y(\sigma)\ge\log Y$.

There is a sequence $\varepsilon_Y\to0$, independent of $S_Y$ in the subcritical range,
such that uniformly over those cells
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(
 \mathcal L(\mathrm{Cl}(D)[\ell^\infty]\mid\Sigma_Y=\sigma,\text{2-data}),
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{CL},\ell}\right)
 \le \varepsilon_Y+O(\Delta_Y(S_Y)),
\]
and the analogous bound holds for every fixed Cohen--Lenstra moment.  In particular,
for every $\eps>0$, the conclusion is uniform whenever
\[
 H_Y\le(1-\eps)\log Y,
 \qquad \log M_Y\le(\tfrac12-\eps)\log Y,
 \qquad \Delta_Y(S_Y)=o(1).
\]
Since every odd prime contributes at most $\log3\le\log p$ of cell entropy,
$H_Y\le\log M_Y$ throughout, so
the conductor constraint is the binding one in the displayed range, while the entropy
governs the impossibility clause below.
At $H_Y\ge(1+\eps)\log Y$ no uniform statement is possible.  Up to the conductor scale
at which $\Delta_Y$ becomes macroscopic, every first-order failure of local independence
factors through this discriminant-cell discrepancy, and there is no earlier
odd-class-group obstruction.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
Wood's framework treats fixed local conditions \cite{Wood}.  The new statement proposes
the full information capacity of an odd class group under a growing local sigma-algebra,
with the entropy governing impossibility and the conductor governing
equidistribution.  It would transfer directly
to Selmer groups, ray class groups, and unramified extension statistics.  The first
theorem is uniformity for $|S_Y|\to\infty$ with $M_Y=Y^{o(1)}$.

\section{Programme III: finite logarithms, regulators, and algebraic tori}

For $p\nmid a$, write
\[
 q_p(a)=\frac{a^{p-1}-1}{p}\pmod p.
\]
For a generating tuple $g=(g_1,\ldots,g_s)$, let $R(g)$ be its multiplicative
relation lattice and let $\T_g\subset(\R/\Z)^s$ be the identity component of its
annihilator.  Its dimension is the saturated multiplicative rank.

\begin{conjecture}[Kummer--Haar law on relation tori]\label{c11}
Let $\Gamma\le\Q^\times$ be finitely generated, saturated, and torsion-free, and let
$g$ be any ordered generating tuple.  As $p$ varies in every fixed compatible
cyclotomic--Kummer Chebotarev class, the vectors
\[
 \frac{(q_p(g_1),\ldots,q_p(g_s))}{p}\in(\R/\Z)^s
\]
equidistribute with Haar measure on $\T_g$.  More strongly, for every finite collection
of compatible Chebotarev restrictions, the conditional empirical measures converge to
the Haar disintegration on the corresponding components, and the support is contained in no
proper closed coset of $\T_g$.  The law is invariant under changing generators and
depends on $\Gamma$ only through its saturation and the fixed Kummer conditioning data.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
Katz formulated horizontal Wieferich equidistribution principles for algebraic groups,
and Shparlinski proved estimates in different horizontal and averaged regimes
\cite{Katz,Shparlinski}.  The new content here is the exact saturated relation torus,
full generator invariance, and disintegration under fixed Kummer classes.  It turns
multiplicative rank into a geometric support theorem rather than a heuristic count of
coordinates.

\begin{conjecture}[Rank-dimensional large sieve for finite logarithms]\label{c12}
Choose a Smith-normal-form basis of the character lattice
$X^*(\T_g)=\Z^s/R(g)^{\mathrm{sat}}$.  There is a constant $\delta_\Gamma>0$ such that,
for every $\eps>0$, every $M\le X^{\delta_\Gamma}$, and all complex weights $a_p$
supported on $X<p\le2X$,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{m\in X^*(\T_g)\\\|m\|_\infty\le M}}
 \left|\sum_{X<p\le2X}a_p
 e\!\left(\frac{\langle m,q_p(g)\rangle}{p}\right)\right|^2
 \ll_{\Gamma,\eps}(M^{\rank\Gamma}+\pi(X))X^\eps\sum_p|a_p|^2.
\]
The same inequality holds after every fixed compatible cyclotomic--Kummer restriction,
and for matrix-valued weights with the Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  The exponent
$\delta_\Gamma$ is invariant under changing the generating tuple.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This is the quantitative engine behind \cref{c11}.  It predicts that saturated rank is
not only the support dimension but the exact dual dimension in a prime-varying large
sieve.  A proof for any positive power range would control genuine shrinking targets
and would be far beyond currently available fixed-frequency tests.  Failure would reveal
an unrecognized spacing or energy obstruction among finite-logarithm vectors.

\begin{conjecture}[Mesoscopic-to-lattice shrinking-target transition]\label{c13}
Let $\Gamma\le\Q^\times$ be saturated and torsion-free of rank $r$, and choose a
basis of $\Gamma$, identifying its relation torus with $(\R/\Z)^r$.  For integers $w_p$ with $1\le w_p=o(p)$, let $E_p(w)$ be the event
that every coordinate of the Fermat-quotient vector has a representative of absolute
value at most $w_p$.

\emph{Mesoscopic universality.}  If $w_p\to\infty$, then in every range in which the
Haar mass
\[
 \lambda(X)=\sum_{p\le X}\left(\frac{2w_p+1}{p}\right)^r
\]
tends to infinity, the count of $E_p(w)$ is asymptotic to $\lambda(X)$.  On prime
blocks with bounded Haar mass, the randomly translated event process converges to
a Poisson process with that mass.

\emph{Lattice transition.}  For every fixed finite target set
$B\subset\Z^r$, there is an arithmetic regulator intensity
$\kappa_{\Gamma,B}\ge0$ such that
\[
 \#\{p\le X:q_p|_\Gamma\in B\}
 =(\kappa_{\Gamma,B}+o(1))\sum_{p\le X}p^{-r}
\]
whenever the sum diverges, while only finitely many such primes occur when $r\ge2$.
As a separately falsifiable genericity clause, for Kummer-generic $\Gamma$ and $B=\{0\}$ one has $\kappa_{\Gamma,B}=1$.  The transition from
Haar universality to the arithmetic intensity occurs only when $w_p$ remains bounded.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This separates two universality classes that a single undifferentiated model
conflates.  Growing targets
are controlled by Haar geometry, while exact Wieferich targets can carry a genuinely
arithmetic regulator intensity.  Gras's much rarer fixed-base model and the Haar model
therefore disagree precisely at the lattice boundary \cite{Gras,BGKK}.  The first
theorem is mesoscopic universality for $w_p=p^\eta$ with one rank-one base.

For a $p$-adic unit $a$, write $\langle a\rangle_p$ for the Teichm\"uller lift of
its residue, and for a prime $\mathfrak p\mid p$ of a number field write
$\tau_{\mathfrak p}$ for the Teichm\"uller lift in the completion at
$\mathfrak p$.  Define
$ q_{p,k}(a)=p^{-1}\log(a\langle a\rangle_p^{-1})\pmod{p^k}$.  The matrix entries
below are the $\mathfrak p$-adic analogues of $q_{p,k}$.

\begin{conjecture}[Horizontal $p$-adic regulator-matrix law]\label{c14}
Let $K/\Q$ be a fixed Galois number field and let
$\Gamma\le\mathcal O_K^\times$ be a saturated subgroup of rank $s$, generated by
$\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_s$.  For a rational prime $p$ splitting completely in
$K$, order the primes $\mathfrak p\mid p$ by the Galois action and define the finite-log
matrix to depth $k$ by
\[
 \mathcal L_{p,k}(\Gamma)=
 \left(
 p^{-1}\log_{\mathfrak p}
   (\varepsilon_j\tau_{\mathfrak p}(\bar\varepsilon_j)^{-1})
       \bmod p^k
 \right)_{\mathfrak p\mid p,\,1\le j\le s}.
\]
It lies in the exact linear relation module $\mathscr R_{\Gamma,k}$ imposed by the norm,
the Galois action, and the integral multiplicative relations in $\Gamma$.

As $p$ varies over rational primes splitting completely in $K$, the normalized matrices
$\mathcal L_{p,1}/p$ equidistribute with Haar measure on the real relation torus attached
to $\mathscr R_{\Gamma,1}$, and the depth-$k$ lifts are Haar on
$\mathscr R_{\Gamma,k}$.  More generally, if $\mathcal D$ is a determinantal or Smith
stratum whose reduction is transverse to the relation module and has codimension $c$,
then the primes for which $\mathcal L_{p,k}$ lies in $\mathcal D$ obey the corresponding
Haar local density.  For codimension-one strata, the point process in $\log\log p$ converges after a
uniform random translation to the Poisson law with the Haar intensity.  Codimension at
least two gives a finite exceptional set with the exact small-prime atoms predicted by
the local measures.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This turns mod-$p$ Leopoldt phenomena into a horizontal random-matrix problem with the
correct global relation module built in.  Determinantal codimension, rather than the
number of displayed units, controls rare regulator defects, while the Smith strata retain
higher $p$-adic depth.  Existing work supplies the regulator and random-matrix languages
separately \cite{BGKK,WangStanley,VanPeski}.  Their horizontal arithmetic
identification is the new content.  The first theorem is the rank-one real-quadratic split-prime law.  The companion diagnostic verifies the norm relation exactly and finds fixed-frequency Fourier magnitudes at most $0.020$ for $\Q(\sqrt2)$, while making no claim about the matrix law.

\begin{conjecture}[Functorial horizontal finite logarithms on algebraic tori]\label{c15}
Let $T/\Q$ be an algebraic torus, let $\mathcal T$ be its smooth integral model away from
a finite set, and let $\Gamma\le T(\Q)$ be finitely generated.  Let $S$ be the identity
component of the Zariski closure of $\Gamma$.  At a good unramified prime $p$, let
$\tau_p(\bar P)$ be the canonical prime-to-$p$ torsion lift of the reduction of
$P\in\Gamma$ and define
\[
 \ell_p(P)=p^{-1}\log_T\bigl(P\tau_p(\bar P)^{-1}\bigr)\pmod p
 \in\operatorname{Lie}(S)(\Fq_p).
\]
For any generators $P_1,\ldots,P_s$ of $\Gamma$, the normalized matrix
$(\ell_p(P_i)/p)_i$ equidistributes, in every compatible Frobenius class, with Haar
measure on the real relation subtorus cut out jointly by the algebraic subgroup $S$ and
the integral relations among the $P_i$.  For every morphism of tori $\phi:T\to T'$,
\[
 \ell_p(\phi(P))=d\phi(\ell_p(P)),
\]
and the horizontal Haar measures push forward under the induced map.  Isogenies preserve
the shrinking-target intensities after the explicit finite kernel correction.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
Katz already proposed Wieferich equidistribution beyond $\mathbb G_m$ \cite{Katz}.  The
additional content is a matrix-valued law for a finitely generated global subgroup,
relation-subtorus support, fixed-Frobenius disintegration, and exact functoriality under
morphisms and isogenies.  It turns horizontal finite logarithms into a functor on the
category of tori with arithmetic subgroups.

\section{Programme IV: arboreal arithmetic dynamics}

For a degree-$d$ map $f$ and basepoint $a$, write
$K_n=\Q(f^{-n}(a))$, $G_n=\Gal(K_n/\Q)$, and let
$\mu_n(C)=|C|/|G_n|$ on conjugacy classes.  For primes $p\le X$ unramified in $K_n$,
let $\widehat\mu_{n,X}$ be the empirical Frobenius measure.

\begin{conjecture}[Entropy--conductor universality of arboreal resolution]\label{c16}
Fix $0<\eta<1$.  For a one-parameter family $\mathcal F$ of non-PCF (not postcritically finite)
degree-$d$ maps,
let $G_{n,f}$ and $\mu_{n,f}$ be the level-$n$ arboreal quotient and its Haar
conjugacy-class measure for the parameter $f$.  Sample parameters of height at most $B$
and define
\[
 R_{\mathcal F}(B,X;\eta)=\max\left\{n:
 \E_{f\in\mathcal F(B)}
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\widehat\mu_{n,X,f},\mu_{n,f})\le\eta\right\}.
\]
Assume the generic arboreal image is open, exceptional parameters have density zero,
GRH holds for the preimage fields, and $(B,X,n)$ lies in the family large-sieve range of
\cref{c17}.  Then
\[
 R_{\mathcal F}(B,X;\eta)=\log_d\log X+O_{\mathcal F,\eta}(1).
\]
The $O(1)$ term is determined by the limiting R\'enyi-entropy profile of the nonuniform
class measures and by the degree-normalized root-discriminant rate
$d^{-n}[K_{n,f}:\Q]^{-1}\log|D_{K_{n,f}}|$, which stays bounded because
$\log|D_{K_{n,f}}|/[K_{n,f}:\Q]$ grows like the critical-orbit height scale
$d^{n}$, whereas $\log|D_{K_{n,f}}|$ itself is doubly exponential in $n$.

If two such families have the same degree, open generic images, uniformly comparable
degree-normalized root-discriminant rates, and boundedly different class-measure R\'enyi profiles,
then their observable depths differ by $O(1)$.  In particular passage to a fixed-index
open subgroup changes the resolution boundary by only a bounded number of levels.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
The conjecture identifies a phase invariant of an infinite Galois representation: how
many levels can be reconstructed from primes below $X$.  The leading
$\log_d\log X$ scale comes from doubly exponential tree complexity, while the bounded
shift records group index and ramification.  Large arboreal images and effective
Chebotarev are inputs, not the conclusion \cite{JonesSurvey,Kadets,ThornerZaman}.

\begin{conjecture}[Arboreal family large sieve at growing depth]\label{c17}
Let $\mathcal F\to\mathbb A^1$ be a generically finite-index arboreal family, and let
$\mathfrak q_n$ be the conductor of the finite \`etale cover over the parameter line that
records the level-$n$ preimage tree.  For a good prime $p$, a conjugacy class
$C\subset G_n$, and a parameter $c$, put
\[
 \Theta_{p,C}(c)=\mathbf1_{\mathrm{Frob}_{p,c}\in C}-\mu_n(C).
\]
For every $\eps>0$ and all complex coefficients $\alpha_{p,C}$,
\[
 \sum_{|c|\le B}
 \left|\sum_{p\le X}\sum_{C\subset G_n}
 \alpha_{p,C}\Theta_{p,C}(c)\right|^2
 \ll_{\eps,\mathcal F}
 \bigl(B+X^2\mathfrak q_n^{1+\eps}\bigr)
 \sum_{p,C}|\alpha_{p,C}|^2\mu_n(C),
\]
uniformly for $n$ with $X^2\mathfrak q_n^{1+\eps}\le B^{1-\eps}$.  The same bound holds
for bounded cylinder functions involving every level $j\le n$.

In the critical range where the analytic Chebotarev error is $o(1)$ but the iid sampling
functional of $\mu_n$ is nonvanishing, the complete conjugacy-cylinder count vector,
averaged over parameters, has the nonuniform multinomial occupancy law of independent
samples from $\mu_n$, including its missing-mass and collision profile.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This is the quantitative arithmetic engine missing from purely group-theoretic
resolution heuristics.  It extends the Frobenius large-sieve philosophy of Kowalski to
growing arboreal quotients and simultaneously controls parameter and prime dependence
\cite{KowalskiSieve}.  A counterexample would exhibit systematic cross-prime or
cross-parameter entanglement invisible in the generic arboreal group.

\begin{conjecture}[Coloured dynatomic Galois-factor process]\label{c18}
For $f_c(x)=x^d+c$, let the critical exact-period polynomial factor as
$\Psi_n(c)=\prod_{j=1}^{s_n}F_{n,j}(c)$ over $\Z$.  Sample $c$ from a dyadic height
interval away from PCF, discriminant, and fixed-resultant loci.

For every fixed $n$, condition on any fixed finite local valuation state and normalize
the prime factors of each $F_{n,j}(c)$ by that component's residual logarithmic mass.
The component-coloured factor processes converge jointly to independent
Frobenius-marked Poisson--Dirichlet $\PD(1)$ processes, with the mark in colour $j$ given by the
fixed-point-size-biased conjugacy law in the splitting field of $F_{n,j}$.  The total
logarithmic mass of ramified factors tends to zero.

Uniformly for $n=n(H)$ with $\deg\Psi_n=o(\log H)$, every fixed correlation functional
whose support lies inside the available level of distribution converges to the
corresponding restricted-support correlation of these coloured marked processes.  No
full-process claim is made at growing degree without level-one distribution.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
Exact-period components are genuine geometric colours, and their Galois marks distinguish
dynamical families with similar unmarked factor sizes.  The statement deliberately
separates the full fixed-degree process from the restricted-support growing-degree law,
in accordance with the distribution thresholds in Bharadwaj--Rodgers \cite{BR}.  The
first theorem is a two-colour correlation law at one fixed period.

\begin{conjecture}[Primitive valuation process for dynatomic values]\label{c19}
For every level $n$, first remove from the exact-period polynomial $\Psi_n$ the canonical
greatest common divisor in $\Q[c]$ with the earlier critical-orbit product
$\prod_{m<n}f_c^m(0)$, and call the resulting polynomial $\Psi_n^{\rm new}$.  For each
prime $p$, let $\nu_{n,p}$ be the exact Haar law on $\Z_p$ of the primitive valuation
\[
 V_{n,p}(c)=v_p(\Psi_n^{\rm new}(c))\,
 \mathbf1_{p\nmid f_c^m(0)\ \forall m<n}.
\]
All discriminant, resultant, and collision primes are carried explicitly by these
exact local laws.

If $n=n(H)$ satisfies $\deg\Psi_n=o(\log H)$ and the local squarefree product is
nonzero, then the full vector $(V_{n,p}(c))_p$, for $c$ in a dyadic height interval,
converges uniformly in $n$ to the adelic product $\bigotimes_p\nu_{n,p}$ in the topology
generated by bounded cylinder functions and the squarefull-mass functional
\[
 \sum_p(\log p)\max(V_{n,p}-1,0).
\]
Consequently the new primitive part is squarefree with probability
$\prod_p\nu_{n,p}(\{0,1\})+o(1)$, and its logarithmic squarefull mass converges to the
compound-Bernoulli law determined by the complete local measures.  The same process law
is uniform under fixed algebraic specializations that preserve the local product.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This predicts the complete primitive valuation process, not merely one squarefree
probability.  The level dependence is essential: for fixed $p$, primitive hits occur by
a bounded time modulo $p^k$.  Exact bad-prime and collision behaviour is retained in the
local measures, while only genuine polynomial common factors are removed canonically.
The process supplies the multiplicity input needed for dynatomic factor laws.  The nearest literature treats primitive divisors and fixed-polynomial
squarefree phenomena, not this uniform adelic process \cite{Primitive,KrummDynatomic}.

\begin{conjecture}[Divisor-sensitive classification of dynamical gcd height]\label{c20}
Let $F=(f,g)$ be a split map on $(\mathbb P^1)^2$, with $f,g$ disintegrated (in the sense of Medvedev and Scanlon
\cite{MedvedevScanlon}, so neither is
linearly conjugate to a power map, a Chebyshev map, or a close relative of these) of
the same degree, and let $P=(a,b)$ have positive canonical heights.  Let $D_1,D_2$ be the two
coordinate-zero divisors and let $E$ be the exceptional divisor of the blow-up at
$D_1\cap D_2$.  Then
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{h_E(F^n(P))}{\max\{h(f^n(a)),h(g^n(b))\}}>0
\]
if and only if there exist $m\ge0$ and an irreducible $F$-periodic curve $Z$ containing
$F^m(P)$ such that, on the normalization of $Z$, the pullbacks of $D_1$ and $D_2$ have a
common nonzero effective component.  In the absence of such eventual entry into a
divisor-compatible periodic curve, the normalized gcd height tends to zero.

The converse is unconditional in every setting where the required Vojta inequality for
the blow-up is known, and otherwise is explicitly conditional on that inequality.  The
same criterion extends to split maps on $(\mathbb P^1)^r$ by replacing a common component
with positive codimension-one intersection multiplicity among the pulled-back divisors.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
The obstruction is not merely a common dynamical quotient.  It is a periodic geometric
relation carrying the actual divisors measured by the gcd height.  Eventual entry is the right
notion, since a preperiodic tail can carry the divisor relation before periodicity
begins.  Recent work resolves several set-theoretic
dynamical-GCD problems, while this height-theoretic divisor classification is the
additional boundary \cite{YangZhong}.  Failure would point to a new source of
macroscopic Diophantine proximity outside invariant geometry.

\section{Programme V: adelic factorization and sieve flow}

Throughout this programme $P^+(m)$ and $P^-(m)$ denote the largest and smallest prime
factors of $m$, the constant $\gamma$ is Euler's, $\rho_f(p)$ counts the roots
of $f$ modulo $p$, and every sample $n\in[N,2N]$ is drawn uniformly.

\begin{conjecture}[Frobenius-marked Poisson--Dirichlet process]\label{c21}
Let $f\in\Z[x]$ be irreducible with splitting field $L$ and transitive Galois group
$G$.  Sample $n\in[N,2N]$.  Mark each unramified prime factor $p$ of $f(n)$ by its
conjugacy class $C\subset G$ and give it mass $u=\log p/\log|f(n)|$.  The macroscopic
marked process converges to a marked $\PD(1)$ process with conditional mark law
\[
 \Pp(C\mid u)=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\operatorname{fix}(C),
\]
independent of $u$.  Given the masses and every fixed finite local valuation state, the
marks of finitely many macroscopic factors are asymptotically independent.  Under a
normal quotient equipped with the induced permutation representation, the marked
process pushes forward functorially.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
Chebotarev contributes $|C|/|G|$, divisibility by a polynomial value size-biases by the
number of fixed roots, and Burnside's lemma normalizes the result.  Bharadwaj--Rodgers
supply the unmarked factor-process framework \cite{BR}.  Simultaneous Galois marking,
conditional independence, and functorial pushforward are the new content.  This is the
cleanest bridge in the collection between Galois theory and probabilistic factorization.

\begin{conjecture}[Three-scale factorization of polynomial values]\label{c22}
Let $f\in\Z[x]$ be irreducible, sample $n\in[N,2N]$, choose
$y=y(N)\to\infty$ with $\log y=o(\log N)$, and write
$R_y=f(n)/S_y(f(n))$, where $S_y(m)=\prod_{p\le y}p^{v_p(m)}$ is the $y$-smooth
part.  Jointly with the complete small-prime valuation field
$(v_p(f(n)))_{p\le y}$, the residual logarithmic factor process
\[
 \sum_{p\mid R_y}v_p(R_y)\,
 \delta_{\left(\log p/\log|R_y|,\,\mathrm{Frob}_p\right)}
\]
converges to the following conservation-corrected product object: the small valuations
have their polynomial Kubilius law, and conditional on their consumed logarithmic mass
the residual process is a scale-invariant Poisson process of intensity $du/u$, marked by
\cref{c21}, conditioned to have total mass one.  The small field and the unconditioned
residual Poisson process are asymptotically independent, and all leading dependence
after conditioning is the single residual-mass constraint.

For disjoint normalized size bands bounded away from zero, the unconditioned residual
process has independent increments.  Its mesoscopic restriction and its ranked
macroscopic atoms yield respectively the scale-invariant factor process and the
Frobenius-marked $\PD(1)$ partition.  Thus the local Kubilius field is the boundary
variable fixing the available mass, while the mesoscopic and macroscopic laws are two
projections of one residual process, and no additional cross-scale coupling survives.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
Raw small--large independence is false because small factors consume mass.  This
conjecture identifies the complete conservation-corrected product structure and adds the
mesoscopic scale that is absent from a two-scale statement.  It would justify, in one
limit theorem, the local sieve model, the scale-invariant factor process, and the
macroscopic partition.

\begin{conjecture}[Adelic Gibbs gluing for reducible polynomial values]\label{c23}
Let $f=\prod_{i=1}^s g_i$ be squarefree with irreducible components, sample
$n\in[N,2N]$, and put $t=n/N$.  For each prime $p$, let $\nu_p$ be the exact Haar law on
$\Z_p$ of the valuation vector
$(v_p(g_1(x)),\ldots,v_p(g_s(x)))$.  For
$y\to\infty$ with $\log y=o(\log N)$, consider stable convergence jointly with the
archimedean coordinate $t$ and condition on
$\mathscr L_y=(v_p(g_i(n)))_{p\le y,i\le s}$.  After normalizing each component by its
own residual logarithmic mass, the coloured Frobenius-marked factor processes converge
to conditionally independent copies of the three-scale law of \cref{c22}, with the
marks of \cref{c21}.

Unconditionally, the joint process is the projective Gibbs mixture obtained by
integrating those conditional product laws against Lebesgue measure in $t$ and the
adelic local law $\bigotimes_p\nu_p$, in the iterated order $N\to\infty$ and then
$y\to\infty$.  Every bounded finite-dimensional Laplace functional is determined by
\[
 Z_p(\mathbf z)=\int_{\Z_p}\prod_i z_i^{v_p(g_i(x))}\,dx
\]
together with the archimedean size profile $(\log|g_i(Nt)|)_i$.  No additional colour
coupling survives after this adelic--archimedean mixture.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
A scalar Euler factor cannot specify a point process.  This statement gives the full
local-to-global gluing rule and includes the archimedean variable that can couple
component sizes.  It is functorial under multiplying, splitting, or regrouping
components.  A persistent medium-prime or archimedean coupling after the stated mixture
would identify a new nonadelic invariant.

\begin{conjecture}[Full multivariate adelic saddle law for joint smoothness]\label{c24}
Let $f_1,\ldots,f_s\in\Z[x]$ be pairwise coprime and primitive, sample
$n\in[N,2N]$, and put $t=n/N$.  For complex variables $\mathbf s$ and smoothness bounds
$\mathbf y$, define
\[
 \mathcal Z_{p,\mathbf y}(\mathbf s)=
 \int_{\Z_p}p^{-\sum_{i:p\le y_i}(s_i-1)v_p(f_i(x))}\,dx,
 \qquad
 \widehat{\mathcal Z}_{p,\mathbf y}(\mathbf s)=
 \frac{\mathcal Z_{p,\mathbf y}(\mathbf s)}
      {\mathcal Z_{p,\mathbf y}(+\boldsymbol\infty)},
\]
where $\mathcal Z_{p,\mathbf y}(+\boldsymbol\infty)$, the limit as every
$s_i\to+\infty$, is the Haar probability that $p$ divides no $f_i(x)$ with
$p\le y_i$, and the exact multivariate saddle action is
\[
 \Phi_t(\mathbf s)=
 \sum_p\log\widehat{\mathcal Z}_{p,\mathbf y}(\mathbf s)
 +\sum_i(s_i-1)\log|f_i(Nt)|.
\]
The normalization is fixed by the one-variable calibration: for $f(x)=x$ the local
factors $\widehat{\mathcal Z}_{p,y}(s)=(1-p^{-s})^{-1}$ and the action reduce
exactly to the Hildebrand--Tenenbaum saddle for the smooth-number density
\cite{HT}, whereas the
unnormalized probability partition function would insert the spurious sieve factor
$\prod_{p\le y}(1-1/p)$, and the omitted Perron kernel $\prod_i s_i^{-1}$ would
return the cumulative average rather than the local density.
Let $\boldsymbol\sigma_t$ be the unique real saddle in the admissible region.  Define the
joint saddle density by the independently specified inverse-Mellin integral
\[
 \mathscr D_t^{\rm joint}=
 \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^s}
 \int_{\boldsymbol\sigma_t+i\R^s}
 e^{\Phi_t(\mathbf s)}\,d\mathbf s,
\]
with the standard truncated-contour limit, and let
$H_t=\nabla^2\Phi_t(\boldsymbol\sigma_t)$.
Uniformly when every $u_i(t)=\log|f_i(Nt)|/\log y_i$ remains in a fixed compact
saddle-point region,
\[
 \Pp(P^+(f_i(n))\le y_i\ \forall i)
 \sim\int_1^2\mathscr D_t^{\rm joint}\,dt,
\]
and the integral admits the full Gaussian saddle expansion with action
$\Phi_t(\boldsymbol\sigma_t)$ and curvature matrix $H_t$, with
$\mathscr D_t^{\rm joint}$ the local smooth-value density at $t$.

If $\mathscr D_{i,t}^{\rm marg}$ are the one-polynomial saddle densities, then
$\mathscr D_t^{\rm joint}/\prod_i\mathscr D_{i,t}^{\rm marg}$ is asymptotic to the
complete ratio of joint and marginal actions and Hessian determinants.  Its Euler-product
part is the ratio of the exact $p$-adic partition functions evaluated at the joint and
marginal saddles.  This local factor, the saddle displacement, and the mixed curvature
are all indispensable at leading order.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
This repairs the principal weakness of simpler joint-smoothness heuristics: the joint
saddle and its curvature cannot be replaced by a scalar Euler correction evaluated at
marginal saddles.  The conjecture combines exact higher $p$-adic valuations, moving
archimedean sizes, and multivariate saddle geometry.  Even the one-polynomial theory is
difficult \cite{MartinSmooth,Mine}, and the full joint adelic action would be a major
new local--global bridge.

\begin{conjecture}[Buchstab--Bateman--Horn component flow]\label{c25}
Let $f\in\Z[x]$ be primitive, irreducible, and admissible, and sample
$n\in[N,2N]$.  For $y$ with
$u_n=\log|f(n)|/\log y$ in a fixed compact subset of $(1,\infty)$, let
$\Omega_y(f(n))$ be the number of prime factors of $f(n)$ exceeding $y$, counted with
multiplicity, on the event $P^-(f(n))>y$.  Put
\[
 V_f(y)=\prod_{p\le y}\left(1-\frac{\rho_f(p)}p\right).
\]
Define the Buchstab component functions by
\[
 \omega_1(u)=\frac1u,
 \qquad
 \omega_j(u)=\frac{1}{u(j-1)!}
 \int_{\substack{t_1,\ldots,t_{j-1}\ge1\\
 t_1+\cdots+t_{j-1}\le u-1}}
 \frac{dt_1\cdots dt_{j-1}}{t_1\cdots t_{j-1}}
 \quad(j\ge2),
\]
so that $\sum_{j\ge1}\omega_j(u)=\omega(u)$.  Then for every fixed $j$,
\[
 \Pp\bigl(P^-(f(n))>y,\ \Omega_y(f(n))=j\bigr)
 =V_f(y)e^\gamma\E_{n\in[N,2N]}\omega_j(u_n)+o(V_f(y)),
\]
uniformly on compact $u$-ranges.  Conditional on the ordered logarithmic sizes of the
$j$ remaining factors, their unramified Frobenius marks are independent with the
fixed-point-size-biased law of \cref{c21}.  Summing over $j$ gives the rough-value
Buchstab law, $j=1$ gives the Bateman--Horn prime density, and $j=2$ gives the
semiprime transition.  Whenever $\max_{N\le n\le2N}|f(n)|<y^2$, every rough value has exactly one prime
factor above $y$, so the $j=1$ component exhausts the rough set, while the density
formula itself remains conjectural.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Why this is top-tier.}
The conjecture resolves a rough polynomial value into its complete finite-$u$ factor
count and Galois mark content.  Primes and semiprimes are not inserted as separate
heuristics.  They are components of one universal flow.  Generalized Buchstab equations
are the random-integer antecedent \cite{Moree}, and the fixed-polynomial, locally
corrected, Galois-marked component law is the new statement.  It is strongly falsifiable even when
total roughness follows Buchstab.

\section{Adversarial quality audit}

The set is intentionally not a list of equally likely claims, but every numbered entry
passes the same top-tier gate.  Every numbered statement except \cref{c2,c5} is a parent mechanism, while those two
are classification laws with independent content rather than generic consequences.

The first decisive theorem for each programme is deliberately much smaller than the full
conjecture:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute the complete connected third cumulant for two pair motifs.
\item Prove the two-point connected arrival kernel $K_2$ and its additive polyspectrum.
\item Prove a rank-one positive-power finite-logarithm large sieve.
\item Prove the arboreal family large sieve at one growing quadratic level.
\item Prove the two-component case of \cref{c23} and the $j=2$ Buchstab
component in an averaged polynomial family.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Novelty caution.}
The literature screening establishes nearest visible boundaries, not priority.  In
particular, Katz already proposed broad algebraic-group Wieferich equidistribution,
Bharadwaj--Rodgers already provide the unmarked large-factor process under distribution
hypotheses, Wood already treats fixed local class-group conditions, and recent work on
dynamical gcds is rapidly developing.  The claimed additional content is therefore the
specific relation-torus, marked, process-level, growing-condition, or classification
structure stated above.
