\large Canonical mechanisms, phase laws, and targeted diagnostics}

\begin{abstract}
This manuscript gives exactly twenty-five candidate conjectures in five programmes:
connected prime-pattern fields, arithmetic occupancy and class groups, finite logarithms
and algebraic tori, arboreal arithmetic dynamics, and marked factorization processes.
Each conjecture names a probability
space or deterministic averaging operation, identifies a structural mechanism, states a
failure mode, and records what a reproducible finite computation does and does not test.
The collection is literature-screened but does not claim professionally certified
priority.  Its aim is to offer conjectures whose resolution would expose new invariants,
new local-to-global bridges, or new phase boundaries rather than merely add further
special cases of standard heuristics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Admission standard}

A conjecture is admitted here only when it satisfies five requirements.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.7em]
\item \textbf{Canonical formulation.}  The objects, averaging law, normalization and
limit are fixed before the data are inspected.
\item \textbf{Mechanism.}  The statement identifies an arithmetic or geometric source,
not merely a Gaussian, Poisson, Gumbel, Bateman--Horn or Poisson--Dirichlet conclusion.
\item \textbf{Discrimination.}  At least two plausible models give different answers.
\item \textbf{Leverage.}  Truth would organize a family of problems, and falsity would
identify a missing invariant rather than merely alter a fitted constant.
\item \textbf{Proof interface.}  The statement exposes a first serious intermediate
theorem, such as a connected correlation estimate, a large-sieve inequality, a uniform
Chebotarev range or a local partition-function calculation.
\end{enumerate}

Several structural commitments hold throughout.  Local singular-series
and zero-correlation descriptions are conjectured to \emph{match}, not added as
independent covariance sources.  Motif clusters are connected polymers of arbitrary
size, not connected components of a finite difference graph.  Exceptional zeros are
studied along hypothetical exceptional conductors, since Page's theorem rules out a
positive-density exceptional ensemble.  Class-group conditioning retains a genuine
family and does not fix the prime-discriminant factors.  Toric finite logarithms use
prime-to-$p$ torsion lifts, which makes functoriality possible.  The arboreal resolution
barrier uses total sampling complexity and missing mass rather than any single
coordinate discrepancy.  Dynamical squarefreeness uses level-dependent local
densities rather than a fixed-prime density that must eventually vanish.  Multivariate
smoothness uses exact $p$-adic valuation states rather than overlapping root-count sets.

\paragraph{Evidence convention.}
The companion script \texttt{research\_quality\_25\_experiments.py} is deterministic and designed to finish on ordinary hardware, and the measured run time is recorded in its JSON output.  Its output is a
finite diagnostic, not evidence of proof.  A computation that tests only a definition,
a null case or a fixed-frequency shadow is labelled accordingly.  Numerical ranges are
small relative to every asymptotic below.

\section{Programme I: connected prime-pattern fields}

Let $H\subset\Z$ be a finite admissible motif and put
\[
 \Lambda_H(n)=\prod_{h\in H}\Lambda(n+h),\qquad
 Y_H(x,L)=\sum_n w\!\left(\frac{n-x}{L}\right)
 \bigl(\Lambda_H(n)-\SG(H)\bigr),
\]
where $w$ is a fixed smooth compactly supported function.  The window position $x$ is
sampled from $[X,2X]$ with density $dx/(x\log2)$, and
$X^\eps\le L\le X^{1-\eps}$.  For a finite collection of translated motifs, the
\emph{exact overlap type} records every equality between their shifted prime
constraints.  The connected singular series $\SG^{\mathrm c}(K_1,\dots,K_r)$ of a
tuple of finite sets is obtained from the usual singular series of unions by M\"obius
inversion over set partitions of the index set, so it depends on the tuple and not
only on the union $\bigcup_jK_j$.

\begin{conjecture}[Connected motif cumulant expansion]\label{c1}
Fix $r\ge2$ and admissible motifs $H_1,\dots,H_r$.  The joint cumulant
$\kappa_r(Y_{H_1},\dots,Y_{H_r})$ has a uniform asymptotic expansion indexed by exact
overlap types $\tau$ of $r$ translated motifs.  The term attached to $\tau$ is the sum,
over all translation vectors realizing $\tau$, of
\[
  W_\tau(X,L;\mathbf t)\,
  \SG^{\mathrm c}\!\left(H_1+t_1,\dots,H_r+t_r\right)
  \prod_{v}(\log X)^{m_v-1},
\]
where $m_v$ is the multiplicity of the prime constraint at the union point $v$ and
$W_\tau$ is the exact intersection weight of the windows.  For every fixed truncation
of the ordering by powers of $L$ and $\log X$, the remainder is smaller than the last
retained scale, uniformly in the stated mesoscopic range.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
This turns the Hardy--Littlewood singular series into a connected generating functional
for a multitype arithmetic field.  The overlap poset records equal prime constraints,
while partition M\"obius inversion records genuine many-body interaction.  Neither
structure alone is sufficient.  The first decisive theorem would establish the formula
for third cumulants of two distinct pair motifs.  Montgomery--Soundararajan moment
methods and Kuperberg's odd-moment analysis are the closest antecedents
\cite{MS,Kuperberg}, with the singular-series sums of Kuperberg, Rodgers and
Roditty-Gershon in number fields as the multitype precedent \cite{KRR}.

\paragraph{Finite diagnostic.}
For four pair fields below $3\cdot10^6$, standardized fourth connected cumulants remain
of moderate size as the window mean grows.  This checks neither the coefficients nor
the uniform asymptotic.  It only shows that the proposed cumulant object is numerically
stable enough to study.

\begin{conjecture}[Overlap-rank spectral filtration]\label{c2}
Let $H_1,\dots,H_m$ be fixed motifs and $Y_c=\sum_i c_iY_{H_i}$.  For every
shared-point rank $j\ge1$, let $A_j$ be the limiting overlap form obtained by summing
the connected local weights over translations for which two motifs share exactly $j$
prime constraints.  Put
\[
  \mathcal W_j=\bigcap_{k>j}\ker A_k.
\]
Then the covariance operator has a complete asymptotic spectral filtration: on every
nonzero quotient $\mathcal W_j/\mathcal W_{j-1}$,
\[
  \frac{\Var(Y_c)}{L(\log X)^j}
\]
converges to a nondegenerate quadratic form induced by $A_j$.  On the terminal space
$\bigcap_{j\ge1}\ker A_j$, the covariance divided by its renormalized disjoint-kernel
scale converges to a nondegenerate limit.  Thus no asymptotic eigenmode can have a
variance scale strictly between two adjacent nonzero levels of this filtration.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight and proof interface.}
This is stronger than reading off the largest overlap in one variance formula.  It says
that the overlap poset supplies the complete asymptotic eigenspace decomposition for any
fixed finite motif family.  Balanced statistics can therefore be designed to remove
successive coincidence ranks and expose genuinely disjoint arithmetic correlation.  The
first decisive test is a three-motif family for which one overlap form has a rational
kernel and the next form is nondegenerate on that kernel.  The companion computation
finds two overlap ranks for four pair motifs but, correctly, no artificial kernel in that
example.

\begin{conjecture}[Mesoscopic local--spectral reciprocity]\label{c3}
Remove the exact coincidence terms of \cref{c2}.  For a Schwartz function $\phi$ with
$\phi(0)=0$, define the local quadratic form
\[
 \mathcal Q^{\rm loc}_{ij}(\phi;L)=
 \sum_{t\in\Z}
 \left[L\int_{\R}w(u)w(u+t/L)\,du\right]
 \phi(t/L)\,\SG^{\rm c}(H_i,H_j+t),
\]
The translation sum is finite because the compact support of $w$ makes the window
overlap factor vanish for $|t|\gg L$, and it carries the Montgomery--Soundararajan
logarithmic drift of partial singular-series sums.  Independently,
insert the full explicit formulas for every factor $\Lambda(n+h)$ in the smoothed motif
covariance and let $\mathcal Q^{\rm spec}_{ij}(\phi;X,L)$ be the resulting quadratic
form in the multiple correlations of zeta and Dirichlet-$L$ zeros, using the Mellin
transforms of $w$ and $\phi$.

For every fixed pair of motifs and every bounded set of Schwartz test functions,
\[
 \mathcal Q^{\rm spec}_{ij}(\phi;X,L)
   -\mathcal Q^{\rm loc}_{ij}(\phi;L)
   =o\!\left(\mathcal V_{ij}(X,L)\right)
\]
uniformly for $X^\eps\le L\le X^{1-\eps}$, where $\mathcal V_{ij}$ is the first
nonzero disjoint covariance scale.  The assertion is an equality of the covariance
quadratic forms after common smoothing, not a literal identification of the additive
Fourier variable with zero ordinates.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight, boundary, and failure mode.}
Goldston--Montgomery-type results relate prime variance to zero pair correlation, while
tuple conjectures describe the same covariance through singular series.  The proposed
reciprocity extends that equivalence to products of shifted von Mangoldt functions and
their multiple-zero spectra.  It prevents double-counting local and spectral formulas as
independent sources.  A failure would exhibit a mesoscopic covariance functional visible
in neither language.  The script checks only finite Wiener--Khinchin consistency and the
local input, and cannot test the multiple-zero side.  Chan's refinement of the classical
pair-correlation equivalence is the closest one-field antecedent \cite{Chan}.

\begin{conjecture}[Prime-motif polymer law]\label{c4}
Fix an admissible motif $H$.  On the integer set of occurrence starts, join two starts
when the corresponding translates overlap.  A \emph{polymer} is a finite connected set
$A$ of starts for which the complete union $U_A=\bigcup_{t\in A}(H+t)$ is admissible.
For $R>0$, let $\mathcal F_{X,L,R}$ be the sigma-field, on the random window-position
space, generated by the explicit-formula modes with zero ordinate
$|\gamma|\le RX/L$.

For every fixed polymer radius, conditionally on $\mathcal F_{X,L,R}$ the Palm factorial
moment measures of the occurrence process converge to the Hardy--Littlewood densities of
all complete unions $U_A$, and these finite-radius laws are projectively consistent.
In the successive limits $X\to\infty$, then $R\to\infty$, then polymer radius
$\to\infty$, the Palm cluster is finite almost surely, cluster starts are conditionally
Poisson, and the cluster-start fraction $\theta(H)$, the limiting ratio of the
cluster-start intensity to the occurrence intensity, satisfies
\[
       \theta(H)=\E^{\rm Palm}\bigl[\,|A|^{-1}\bigr],
\]
which equals $1/\E|A|$ for the per-cluster size law, since the occurrence-Palm
cluster-size law is the size-biased version of that law.
Equivalently, the arithmetic polymer partition function is subcritical for every fixed
admissible motif.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
Clusters are not connected components of the finite difference graph $H-H$: for
$H=\{0,6\}$ the starts $0,6,12$ form a connected three-occurrence polymer with admissible
union $\{0,6,12,18\}$, so polymers of every size carry weight.  The explicit-formula
sigma-field fixes the conditioning canonically.  The conjecture predicts the entire Palm cluster law and gives
a concrete proof interface through convergence of polymer partition functions.

\begin{conjecture}[Cycle-rank suppression in prime cumulants]\label{c5}
In the exact connected expansion of \cref{c1}, contract every forced coincidence and
associate to each remaining translation diagram its cyclomatic number $g$, the number of
independent cycles in its overlap hypergraph.  After all larger coincidence scales are
removed, a diagram of cyclomatic number $g$ is smaller than a hypertree diagram with the
same vertices by at least a factor of $(\log L)^{-g}$.  Consequently the first nonzero odd cumulant
of any balanced motif statistic is carried by connected acyclic overlap hypergraphs.
For the single-prime motif this recovers Kuperberg's predicted order
\[
  \kappa_{2m+1}\asymp L^m(\log L)^{m+1}.
\]
As a separately falsifiable sharper layer, in the fixed logarithmic variable
$\log L$ the same quantity is conjectured to obey
$\kappa_{2m+1}=(c_m+o(1))\,L^m(\log L)^{m+1}$ with $c_m$ the total connected
hypertree weight.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
A Gaussian limit only says that normalized odd cumulants vanish.  This conjecture
identifies the topological invariant governing the first non-Gaussian term: every
independent diagram cycle costs one logarithmic order.  It therefore predicts which
subfamilies of singular-series sums must be evaluated and supplies a mechanism for the
odd-moment scale rather than repeating it.  Kuperberg's conjecture and bounds are the
single-field calibration \cite{Kuperberg}.  The motif-dependent cycle filtration is the
new content.

\section{Programme II: arithmetic occupancy and local--global decoupling}

For a prime modulus $q$ and $a\in\Fq_q^\times$, let $p(q,a)$ be the least prime in the
class $a$ and put
\[
             T_q(a)=\frac{\Li(p(q,a))}{q-1}.
\]
The probability space chooses $q$ uniformly among the primes in $[Q,2Q]$ and then
chooses $a$ uniformly from $\Fq_q^\times$.

\begin{conjecture}[Connected occupancy expansion]\label{c6}
For every fixed $R\ge2$ and $t$ in a fixed compact subset of $[0,\infty)$,
\[
 -\log\Pp[T_q(a)>t]
   =t+\sum_{r=2}^{R}\frac{A_r(t)}{(\log Q)^{r-1}}
      +o\bigl((\log Q)^{1-R}\bigr).
\]
The coefficient $A_r$ is the first-arrival transform of the connected $r$-prime
correlation for primes lying in one residue class, averaged over the dyadic modulus
block: writing $w^{\mathrm c}_r(u_1,\dots,u_r)$ for the connected correlation density
of the arrival times under the full $(q,a)$-ensemble, in the normalized clock
$u=\Li(p)/(q-1)$, the exponential formula gives
\[
 -\log\Pp[T_q(a)>t]
   =\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{r-1}}{r!}
    \int_{[0,t]^r}w^{\mathrm c}_r(\mathbf u)\,d\mathbf u,
\]
with $w^{\mathrm c}_1=1$ supplying the leading term $t$, and $A_r(t)$ is the $r$th
term rescaled by $(\log Q)^{r-1}$.  The ensemble is the one fixed above, so the
connected densities include the block-of-modulus cumulants, and no
average-of-logarithms interchange is committed.  No assertion that $A_2$ alone gives the first correction is built into the
definition.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
This is a cluster expansion for an arithmetic coupon collector.  It distinguishes the
prime process from an exchangeable collector and makes higher connected correlations
separately measurable.  Li--Pratt--Shakan introduced the occupancy viewpoint for the
extreme least prime \cite{LPS}.  The new content is the full survival functional.  In the
finite computation, the scaled mean speedup rises from about $1.27$ to $1.60$ across the
sampled modulus bands, while the cover time remains roughly $2$ to $2.5$ below the iid
harmonic benchmark.  The range cannot distinguish $1/\log Q$ from nearby slowly varying
scales.

\begin{conjecture}[Dual autocorrelation laws and the Gauss transfer]\label{c7}
Extend the centred least-prime field to $f_q:\Fq_q\to\R$ by assigning a fixed recorded
value at $0$ and then subtracting its full mean.  Define normalized additive and
multiplicative coefficients
\[
 A_q(\xi)=q^{-1/2}\sum_{a\bmod q}f_q(a)e(-a\xi/q),\qquad
 M_q(\chi)=(q-1)^{-1/2}\sum_{a\ne0}f_q(a)\overline{\chi(a)}.
\]
For every nontrivial character, the exact Gauss identity gives
\[
 M_q(\chi)=\frac{\tau(\overline\chi)}{\sqrt{q(q-1)}}
       \sum_{\xi\ne0}A_q(\xi)\chi(\xi).
\]
The conjectural content is the following pair of compatible laws.

\emph{(i)} For every fixed additive lag $h$, the dyadically averaged autocorrelation
$q^{-1}\sum_a f_q(a)f_q(a+h)$ has a full expansion whose coefficients are the
first-arrival transforms of the connected same-class prime correlations of \cref{c6}.

\emph{(ii)} For every fixed character order and compatible family of characters, the
dyadically averaged energies $|M_q(\chi)|^2$ and their finite joint moments are given by
the low-zero explicit-formula correlations of the corresponding Dirichlet $L$-functions.

These two laws obey the exact Gauss-transfer identity above at every $q$.  No claim that
additive and multiplicative mechanisms are orthogonal or exhaustive is made.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight and diagnostic.}
Additive and multiplicative Fourier systems are not independent coordinate worlds: the
Gauss transform converts one coefficient vector into the other exactly.  The
number-theoretic claim is instead that two different autocorrelation observables isolate
two mechanisms while remaining algebraically compatible.  A projection of the tensor square onto additive-difference and multiplicative-ratio
matrix algebras cannot serve here: computed on the sampled moduli, those combined
algebras leave a relative Hilbert--Schmidt residual above $0.97$ on the centred
tensor square, so neither family, nor both together, spans the observable.  The
script performs this projection, verifies the Gauss identity numerically, and records
the two autocorrelation families separately.

\begin{conjecture}[Terminal environment--polymer process for the last classes]\label{c8}
Let
\[
 b_Q=\inf\bigl\{b:\ \E_{q\in[Q,2Q]}\#\{a:T_q(a)>b\}\le1\bigr\},
\]
the canonical centering for the finite ensemble, and put $\Xi_q=\sum_{a\ne0}\delta_{T_q(a)-b_Q}$.  For $\eta>0$, let
$\mathcal G_{Q,\eta}$ be the sigma-field generated, under the random-modulus law, by
those normalized multiplicative-character coordinates of the arrival field whose
dyadic variance is at least $\eta$.  Define the conditional log-intensity field
\[
 W_{Q,\eta}(x)=\log\E\!\left[\#\{a:T_q(a)>b_Q+x\}\mid
 \mathcal G_{Q,\eta}\right]+x.
\]
First let $Q\to\infty$ and then $\eta\downarrow0$.  The fields
$W_{Q,\eta}$ converge in this successive limit to a random locally bounded field $W$.

Conditionally on $W_{Q,\eta}$, the cluster starts of $\Xi_q$ converge to a Poisson process
with intensity
\[
       \theta_{\rm occ}\exp\{-x+W(x)\}\,dx,
\]
where $\theta_{\rm occ}\in(0,1]$ is the limiting cluster-start fraction of the
terminal process, the analogue of $\theta(H)$ in \cref{c4}.
The within-cluster law is the projective connected-polymer law generated by the terminal
connected occupancy coefficients of \cref{c6}.  Removing the conditioning gives the
corresponding Cox polymer process.  Both the random environment and the cluster law are
part of the limit, and neither is compressed into a fitted location shift.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
The statement predicts the joint law, multiplicities, character profiles, and spacings
of all final residue classes.  It discriminates among an iid coupon collector, a scalar
Cox mixture, and a genuinely field-valued arithmetic environment.  The variance cutoff
above makes the low-mode field canonical rather than selecting modes after inspecting an
extreme.  The implicit centering avoids extrapolating a compact-time expansion to
$t\asymp\log Q$.

\begin{conjecture}[Exceptional-zero hazard transfer]\label{c9}
Suppose that primitive real characters $\chi_j$ of conductors $q_j\to\infty$ have real
zeros $\beta_j$ with $(1-\beta_j)\log q_j\to0$.  Let $x_j(t)$ be defined by
\[
       \Li(x_j(t))/(q_j-1)=t,
\]
and let $S_j^\pm(t)$ be the survival proportions of residue classes with
$\chi_j(a)=\pm1$.  After subtracting the ordinary connected occupancy contribution of
\cref{c6}, put
\[
       H_j(t)=2\int_0^t x_j(u)^{\beta_j-1}\,du.
\]
For every fixed $T>0$,
\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}
 \left|\log S_j^+(t)-\log S_j^-(t)-H_j(t)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Furthermore every character projection orthogonal to $1$ and $\chi_j$ is
$o(\|H_j\|_{C[0,T]})$.  Thus the exceptional explicit-formula term remains rank one
under the nonlinear first-arrival map to leading order.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight and conditional status.}
Page's theorem allows at most one exceptional character below a conductor bound, so no
positive-density exceptional ensemble is available \cite{BasakPratt}.  The formula above
uses a moving, explicitly normalized target rather than saying that a sequence-dependent
quantity ``converges to itself.''  If an exceptional sequence exists, higher harmonics
would pinpoint exactly where nonlinear first-arrival dynamics defeats the rank-one
explicit-formula picture.  If no such sequence exists, the conjecture is intentionally
instanceless and is not counted as evidence for exceptional zeros.

\begin{conjecture}[Entropy--conductor local independence of odd class groups]\label{c10}
Fix an odd prime $\ell$.  Sample negative fundamental discriminants
$D\in[-2Y,-Y]$ and condition on a fixed coarse two-primary invariant.  Let $S_Y$ be a
set of primes not dividing $D$, let $M_Y=\prod_{p\in S_Y}p$, and prescribe an attainable
vector of splitting signs $(D/p)$.  Write $H_Y$ for the logarithm of the number of
attainable sign cells after reciprocity relations are imposed.

Uniformly over cells whose expected size tends to infinity, the conditional law of
$\mathrm{Cl}(D)[\ell^\infty]$ converges in total variation to the unconditioned
Cohen--Lenstra law whenever, for some fixed $\eps>0$,
\[
       H_Y\le(1-\eps)\log Y,
       \qquad \log M_Y\le(\tfrac12-\eps)\log Y.
\]
More generally the same conclusion holds whenever the local-cell entropy and the
analytic conductor complexity jointly lie in the range in which discriminants are
equidistributed among the corresponding sign cells.  At the information boundary
$H_Y\ge(1+\eps)\log Y$, no uniform total-variation assertion is possible.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight and literature boundary.}
The first constraint prevents empty information cells, and the second records that the same
number of sign bits can be imposed at radically different moduli.  The conjecture is
therefore not merely ``independence under many local conditions,'' but a two-resource
local--global principle.  Wood's fixed-local-condition results and conjectures are the
nearest foundation \cite{Wood}.  A proof in any range with $|S_Y|\to\infty$ would be a
substantial advance and would have direct Selmer- and ray-class analogues.

\section{Programme III: finite logarithms, rank and algebraic tori}

For $p\nmid a$, write
\[
      q_p(a)=\frac{a^{p-1}-1}{p}\pmod p.
\]
For a tuple $g=(g_1,\ldots,g_s)$, let $R(g)$ be its multiplicative relation lattice and
let $\T_g\subset(\R/\Z)^s$ be the identity component of its annihilator.  Its dimension
is the saturated multiplicative rank.

\begin{conjecture}[Kummer--Haar law for Fermat quotients]\label{c11}
Let $\Gamma\le\Q^\times$ be saturated of rank $r$, generated by $g$.  As $p$ varies in
any fixed compatible cyclotomic--Kummer Chebotarev class,
\[
        \frac{(q_p(g_1),\ldots,q_p(g_s))}{p}
\]
equidistributes with Haar measure on $\T_g$.  Equivalently, every Fourier coefficient
outside the saturated relation lattice tends to zero, and no proper closed subgroup of
$\T_g$ contains the support.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
The controlling invariant is saturated rank, not the number of displayed bases.  The
statement unifies marginal Fermat-quotient distribution, simultaneous near-zero events
and restrictions to fixed splitting classes.  Existing exponential-sum estimates treat
different horizontal or averaged regimes \cite{Shparlinski}.  The full prime-varying
relation-torus law is substantially stronger.  Below $120000$, exact relations among
bases $2,3,6,12$ hold to machine precision and fixed-frequency Fourier coefficients are
small.  Only the elementary support and fixed-frequency shadows are tested.

\begin{conjecture}[Power-range rank-dimensional large sieve for finite logarithms]\label{c12}
For every saturated $\Gamma\le\Q^\times$ of rank $r$, choose a Smith-normal-form basis
of the character lattice
$X^*(\T_g)=\Z^s/R(g)^{\rm sat}$ and use the associated sup norm.  Changing this basis
alters heights by bounded factors only.  There exists $\delta_\Gamma>0$ such that, for
every $\eps>0$, every $M\le X^{\delta_\Gamma}$, and all complex weights $a_p$
supported on $X<p\le2X$,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{m\in X^*(\T_g)\\ \|m\|_\infty\le M}}
 \left|\sum_{X<p\le2X}a_p
 e\!\left(\frac{\langle m,q_p(g)\rangle}{p}\right)\right|^2
 \ll_{\Gamma,\eps}(M^r+\pi(X))X^\eps\sum_p|a_p|^2.
\]
The estimate remains valid after conditioning on fixed compatible cyclotomic--Kummer
data.  The exponent $\delta_\Gamma$ is positive and invariant under changing the
generating tuple, but no near-full frequency range is assumed.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight and calibration.}
This is the quantitative engine behind the fixed-scale Haar law, with the dual dimension
equal to saturated rank.  Existing Fermat-quotient exponential sums concern different
horizontal or averaged regimes \cite{Shparlinski}.  A positive power range would already
control genuinely shrinking targets.  The explicit character-lattice basis gives the
frequency height a well-defined meaning on the quotient group.

\begin{conjecture}[Shrinking-target phase transition for finite logarithms]\label{c13}
Let $\Gamma\le\Q^\times$ be saturated and torsion-free of rank $r$.  Choose a basis of
its relation torus.  For $0<\alpha<1$, fixed $B>0$, and a prime $p$, let
$E_p(\alpha,B)$ be the event that every coordinate of the Fermat-quotient vector has a
representative of absolute value at most
$\lfloor Bp^{1-\alpha}\rfloor$.  Then
\[
 \#\{p\le X:E_p(\alpha,B)\}
 \sim \sum_{p\le X}
 \left(\frac{2\lfloor Bp^{1-\alpha}\rfloor+1}{p}\right)^r
\]
whenever $\alpha r<1$, whereas only finitely many such primes occur when
$\alpha r>1$.

At the critical line $\alpha r=1$, choose $U_S$ uniformly from $[0,S]$ and put
\[
 \Xi_S=\sum_{\substack{E_p(\alpha,B)\ \text{occurs}\\
                 0\le\log\log p\le S}}
          \delta_{\log\log p-U_S}.
\]
This random translated process converges locally to a Poisson process of intensity
$(2B)^r$.  Separately, the exact target $q_p|_\Gamma=0$ has Haar mass $p^{-r}$: its
$\log\log p$ point process has unit critical intensity at rank one and is finite at rank
at least two.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight and discrimination.}
The conjecture extends \cref{c11} from fixed sets to targets shrinking at a power of the
residue modulus and makes rank the phase-transition invariant.  Exact Wieferich events
are the lattice-scale endpoint, not an isolated folklore assertion.  The Poisson clause
supplies a legitimate probability space by random translation in $\log\log p$-time.  It
sharply distinguishes the Haar shrinking-target model from heuristics predicting much
rarer fixed-base Wieferich events, such as those discussed by Gras \cite{Gras} and, through the mod $p$
Leopoldt lens, by Boeckle, Guiraud, Kalyanswamy and Khare \cite{BGKK}.

\begin{conjecture}[Height-family rank--depth rare-event process]\label{c14}
Choose an ordered $r$-tuple of integers independently and uniformly from
$[-H,H]\setminus\{0\}$, conditioned on multiplicative independence.  For a prime $p$
and $k\ge1$, mark $(p,k)$ when every coordinate is a $p$-adic unit and satisfies
$a^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod{p^{k+1}}$.  Its exact local probability is
\[
       (1-p^{-1})^r p^{-rk},
\]
and, conditional on depth $k$, the probability of depth $k+1$ is $p^{-r}$.

For the critical case $rk=1$, fix $\eps>0$ and form
\[
 \Xi_H=\sum_{p^{k+1}\le H^{1-\eps}}
  \mathbf 1_{(p,k)\ \text{marked}}\,
  \delta_{\log p/\log H}.
\]
On every compact subinterval of $(0,(1-\eps)/(k+1))$, $\Xi_H$ converges to a Poisson
process of intensity $dt/t$.

For $rk>1$, the full marked set converges in finite-prime marginals to the product of the
exact local Bernoulli laws.  After deleting primes $p\le z$ and then taking
$H\to\infty$, its large-prime tail is Poisson with mean
$\sum_{p>z}(1-p^{-1})^rp^{-rk}$, and this mean tends to zero as $z\to\infty$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
No single undifferentiated Poisson process can describe both regimes: the rank-depth
product
$rk=1$ has logarithmically many events, whereas $rk>1$ is a summable collection with
non-negligible small-prime atoms.  The statement gives each regime its natural
limit and retains the exact Haar-lift dependence between depths.  The range
$p^{k+1}\le H^{1-\eps}$ is where the height box genuinely samples the required residue
classes.

\begin{conjecture}[Functorial finite logarithms on algebraic tori]\label{c15}
Let $T/\Q$ be a torus with good unramified reduction at $p$, and let
$P\in T(\Q_p)$ be integral.  The finite group $T(\Fq_p)$ has order prime to $p$.  Let
$\tau_p(\bar P)$ be the unique prime-to-$p$ torsion lift of the reduction of $P$.  Define
\[
 \ell_p(P)=\frac1p\log_T\!\left(P\tau_p(\bar P)^{-1}\right)\pmod p
       \in\operatorname{Lie}(T)(\Fq_p).
\]
If $S$ is the identity component of the Zariski closure of $\langle P\rangle$, then,
after choosing an integral basis of $\operatorname{Lie}(S)$ and dividing its residue
representatives by $p$, the resulting points equidistribute with Haar measure on
$(\R/\Z)^{\dim S}$ as $p$ varies through every compatible Frobenius class.  For every
morphism of unramified tori $\phi:T\to T'$ one has
\[
        \ell_p(\phi(P))=d\phi(\ell_p(P)).
\]
Depth-$k$ vanishing therefore obeys the same codimension phase law as \cref{c13}, with
$r$ replaced by $\dim S$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
Only chosen integer representatives are scaled to the real torus, so no element of
$\operatorname{Lie}(T)(\Fq_p)$ is ever divided by $p$.  Removing the
prime-to-$p$ torsion lift is canonical and functorial, unlike multiplication by unrelated
source and target exponents.  For $\mathbb G_m$ this recovers the Fermat quotient up to
sign.  The script verifies several functorial identities exactly.  The horizontal Haar
law is the conjectural content.

\section{Programme IV: arboreal resolution and dynamical factors}

Let $K_n=\Q(f^{-n}(a))$, $G_n=\Gal(K_n/\Q)$, and let $\mu_n(C)=|C|/|G_n|$ on the
conjugacy classes of $G_n$.  Put $N_X=\pi(X)$ and define the canonical iid sampling
functional by
\[
 \cS(n,X)=\E\,\TV\!\left(\frac1{N_X}\sum_{j=1}^{N_X}\delta_{Z_j},\mu_n\right),
 \qquad Z_j\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mu_n.
\]
It is explicitly computable from $\mu_n$.  The usual bound
$\cS\ll\frac12\sum_C\min\{\mu_n(C),\sqrt{\mu_n(C)/N_X}\}$ is used only as a proxy in
the finite script.  If $k_n$ is the number of conjugacy classes, define the
aggregate conductor functional, a deliberately conservative proxy at the
generalized-Riemann-hypothesis scale,
\[
 \cA(n,X)=
 \frac{\sqrt{k_n|G_n|}\,[\log|D_{K_n}|+[K_n:\Q]\log X]\log X}{\sqrt X}.
\]

\begin{conjecture}[Finite-index universality of arboreal resolution depth]\label{c16}
For an arboreal representation with finite quotient $G_n$, let $\mathcal S_G(n,X)$ be
the expected total variation of the empirical measure of $\pi(X)$ iid samples from Haar
conjugacy-class measure on $G_n$.  Let $\mathcal A_f(n,X)$ be the explicit aggregated
aggregate conductor functional defined immediately above from $k_n$, $|G_n|$, the field
degree, and $\log|D_{K_n}|$.  Define the model resolution depth
\[
 n_f^*(X)=\max\{n:\mathcal S_G(n,X)+\mathcal A_f(n,X)\le1/10\}.
\]
Consider two non-PCF maps of the same degree whose arboreal images are open and whose
preimage-field discriminants have comparable ramification entropy, in the sense that
$\log|D_{K_n^{(1)}}|/\log|D_{K_n^{(2)}}|$ stays between two positive constants.  Then
\[
        n_{f_1}^*(X)-n_{f_2}^*(X)=O(1).
\]
The actual largest level at which empirical Frobenius measures converge in total
variation differs from $n_f^*(X)$ by $O(1)$ in the same family-averaged regime.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
An open subgroup contains a fixed level stabilizer, so finite index changes only bounded
initial tree data, while the doubly exponential state growth fixes the leading observable
depth.  The ramification comparison is essential: group index alone cannot control
discriminants.  This is a universality statement about how much of an infinite Galois
representation is observable, rather than a repackaged effective-Chebotarev implication.
Large arboreal images and effective Chebotarev provide the inputs
\cite{Kadets,ThornerZaman}.  The script compares iid resolution functionals for the full
binary wreath model and an explicit index-two parity subgroup at small levels.

\begin{conjecture}[Critical arboreal occupancy profile]\label{c17}
Sample the parameter uniformly from a one-parameter family of height at most $B$ whose
arboreal images have uniformly bounded index, and let $n=n(B)$ and $X=X(B)$ tend to
infinity.  Suppose the family-averaged analytic Chebotarev error is $o(1)$ in total
variation.  Write
$\mu_n(C)=|C|/|G_n|$ and $N_X=\pi(X)$.  Then the complete conjugacy-cylinder count vector
has the same asymptotic law as the multinomial occupancy vector obtained from $N_X$
independent samples from $\mu_n$.

Equivalently, after Poissonizing the number of sampled primes, counts in classes with
$N_X\mu_n(C)\to\lambda_C$ converge jointly to independent
$\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_C)$ variables, while the empirical missing mass, collision
mass, and total variation converge to the corresponding nonuniform occupancy
functionals.  De-Poissonization recovers the fixed-total multinomial law.  In particular
the total-variation transition occurs exactly when the canonical iid sampling
functional ceases to vanish.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight and risk.}
The conjecture predicts the whole critical profile rather than only a threshold.  The
actual Haar class weights, not $|G_n|$ treated as equiprobable states, determine the
boundary.  Its falsity would reveal prime-to-prime or parameter-to-parameter correlations
surviving after every effective Chebotarev error has been removed.  The companion
calculation evaluates the exact iid missing-mass and total-variation proxies at fixed
small levels.  Growing conjugacy depth remains inaccessible.

\begin{conjecture}[Coloured dynatomic distribution-and-factor process]\label{c18}
For $f_c(x)=x^d+c$, let the critical dynatomic polynomial at level $n$ factor as
$\Psi_n=\prod_{j=1}^{s_n}F_{n,j}$ over $\Z$, and sample $|c|\le H$ away from the PCF,
reducible, discriminant, and fixed-resultant loci.

For every fixed $n$, after conditioning on each fixed finite local state and normalizing
each component by its residual logarithmic mass, the component-coloured prime-factor
processes converge jointly to independent $\PD(1)$ processes.  Unramified factors in
colour $j$ carry the fixed-point-size-biased Frobenius mark in the splitting field of
$F_{n,j}$, and the total logarithmic mass of ramified factors is $o(1)$ in probability.

Uniformly for $n=n(H)$ with $\deg\Psi_n=o(\log H)$, the same conclusion holds at the
level of every fixed correlation functional whose support lies inside the level of
distribution supplied by the exact local root-count tensors of the $F_{n,j}$.  Thus the
fixed-level statement is a full process law, while the growing-level statement makes
only the restricted-support assertion justified by its distribution range.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
The statement separates two mathematically different claims: a small positive
distribution level does not automatically give a full Poisson--Dirichlet process
for growing degree.  Dynatomic components are genuine colours arising from exact-period
geometry, and their Galois marks distinguish families with similar unmarked factor
sizes.  Bharadwaj--Rodgers supply the factor-process template and the distinction between
full and restricted-support conclusions \cite{BR}.  The coloured dynatomic and
growing-degree uniformity are the new claims.

\begin{conjecture}[Uniform squarefree sieve for growing dynatomic degree]\label{c19}
For each $n$ and prime $p$, let
\[
 \delta_{n,p}=p^{-2}\#\{c\bmod p^2:p^2\mid\Psi_n(c)
           \text{ and }p\nmid v_m(c)\text{ for }m<n\}.
\]
Here $v_m(c)=f_c^{m}(0)$ denotes the $m$th critical-orbit value, so the condition
selects primitive divisibility in the sense of the primitive-prime-divisor
literature \cite{Primitive}.  In the regime $\deg\Psi_n=o(\log H)$,
\[
 \Pp_{|c|\le H}\bigl[\Psi_n(c)\text{ is squarefree}\bigr]
   =\prod_p(1-\delta_{n,p})+o(1)
\]
uniformly in $n$, after explicitly deleting the finitely many critical-collision local
states.  Moreover the logarithm of the squarefull part is $o(\log|\Psi_n(c)|)$ in
probability.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
For fixed $p$, a first primitive hit occurs by bounded time modulo $p^2$, so no nonzero
fixed-prime density can stabilize as $n\to\infty$: the local density must depend on
$n$.  For levels $2,3,4$, empirical squarefree fractions
$0.622,0.916,0.987$ compare with local products truncated at $p\le43$, namely $0.611,0.924,0.989$, and the
agreement checks the level-dependent local object at small degree only.

\begin{conjecture}[Divisor-sensitive classification of dynamical gcd height]\label{c20}
Let $f,g$ be disintegrated polynomials of the same degree, let
$F=(f,g)$ on $(\mathbb P^1)^2$, and let $P=(a,b)$ have positive canonical heights.  Let
$E$ be the exceptional divisor of the blow-up at $(0,0)$, so that the generalized
logarithmic gcd is the $E$-height of $F^n(P)$.

The normalized gcd height has positive limsup if and only if, after replacing $F$ by an
iterate, $P$ lies on an $F$-periodic irreducible curve $Z$ such that on the normalization
of $Z$ the pullbacks of the two coordinate-zero divisors share a nonzero effective
component.  In the absence of such a divisor-compatible periodic curve,
\[
 \frac{h_E(F^n(P))}{\max\{h(f^n(a)),h(g^n(b))\}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The converse is asserted in the same range in which the Vojta inequality for the blow-up
controls proximity to $E$.  Without that Diophantine input it is a separately
identified conditional clause.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight and current boundary.}
A periodic correspondence alone is insufficient: it must carry the actual divisors
measured by gcd height.  This formulation joins dynamical unlikely intersections,
blow-up heights, and invariant-curve classification in one obstruction.  Recent work of
Yang--Zhong resolves several set-theoretic common-zero and dynamical-GCD questions
\cite{YangZhong}.  The height-level divisor-compatible if-and-only-if is the proposed
additional boundary, not a restatement of those results.

\section{Programme V: Frobenius-marked factorization and sieve flow}

\begin{conjecture}[Frobenius-marked Poisson--Dirichlet factor process]\label{c21}
Let $f\in\Z[x]$ be irreducible with splitting field $L$ and transitive Galois group
$G$.  Sample $n$ uniformly from a dyadic interval $[N,2N]$.  Mark each unramified prime
factor $p$ of $f(n)$ by its conjugacy class $C\subset G$ and give it mass
$u=\log p/\log|f(n)|$.  The macroscopic process converges to a marked $\PD(1)$ process
with mark law
\[
      \Pp(C)=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\operatorname{fix}(C).
\]
Conditional on the masses and on any fixed finite local valuation state, the marks of
finitely many macroscopic factors are asymptotically independent.  Under a normal
quotient equipped with its induced permutation representation, the marked process
pushes forward functorially.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
Chebotarev supplies $|C|/|G|$, divisibility by a polynomial value size-biases by the
number of fixed roots, and Burnside's lemma normalizes the law.  This joins Galois theory
to the large-factor process.  Bharadwaj--Rodgers prove Poisson--Dirichlet limits or
restricted-support correlations for well-distributed sequences, including irreducible
polynomial values under the relevant distribution hypotheses \cite{BR}.  For
$x^3-2$ and $n\le1600$, the largest-factor marks split $49.75\%$ versus $50.25\%$
between the one- and three-fixed-point classes, close to the size-biased $S_3$ prediction.

\begin{conjecture}[Small--large independence after residual-mass normalization]\label{c22}
Let $y\to\infty$ with $\log y=o(\log N)$.  Write $S_y(f(n))$ for the $y$-smooth part and
$R_y=f(n)/S_y(f(n))$.  The sigma-field generated by
$\{v_p(f(n)):p\le y\}$ is asymptotically independent of the Frobenius-marked large-factor
process of $R_y$ when every large-factor mass is normalized by $\log|R_y|$.  Equivalently,
the joint Laplace functional factors after this residual-mass normalization and after
conditioning on the finite ramification state.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
Raw independence is false because small factors consume mass.  In the finite $x^3-2$
sample, the correlation between the number of factors at most $100$ and the raw largest
share is $-0.434$, falling to about $0.107$ after residual normalization.  The remaining
correlation is large enough that the asymptotic independence is not being assumed from
the data.

\begin{conjecture}[Projective $p$-adic Gibbs gluing for reducible polynomial values]\label{c23}
Let $f=\prod_{i=1}^s g_i$ be squarefree with irreducible components and sample
$n\in[N,2N]$.  For each prime $p$ let $\nu_p$ be the exact probability law of the
valuation vector $(v_p(g_1(x)),\dots,v_p(g_s(x)))$ for Haar-random $x\in\Z_p$, with
partition function
\[
 Z_p(\mathbf z)=\int_{\Z_p}\prod_i z_i^{v_p(g_i(x))}\,dx.
\]
For $y\to\infty$ with $\log y=o(\log N)$, condition on the complete local state
$\mathscr L_y=(v_p(g_i(n)))_{p\le y,i\le s}$ and normalize each component's large factors
by its own residual logarithmic mass.  The coloured marked factor processes are then
asymptotically independent.

Before conditioning, their joint law is the projective Gibbs mixture obtained by
integrating those independent conditional laws against
$\bigotimes_{p\le y}\nu_p$ and then taking $N\to\infty$ followed by $y\to\infty$.
Equivalently every bounded finite-dimensional Laplace functional is the limit of the
corresponding finite-$y$ mixture, and this projective family is completely determined by
the $Z_p$.  No additional colour coupling survives in the limit.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
A scalar Euler factor can correct one avoidance probability but cannot specify a point
process.  The projective Gibbs formulation encodes every local valuation and the random
residual masses they consume.  It supplies a functorial rule under multiplying or
refactoring polynomials.  The strong final sentence is directly falsifiable by a
persistent archimedean or medium-prime colour correlation after the iterated limit.

\begin{conjecture}[Adelic saddle-point law for simultaneous smooth polynomial values]\label{c24}
Let $f_1,\dots,f_s$ be pairwise coprime primitive polynomials and sample
$n\in[N,2N]$.  Put $t=n/N$.  For each prime define the exact local partition function
\[
 Z_{p,\mathbf y}(\boldsymbol\sigma)=
 \int_{\Z_p}p^{-\sum_{i:p\le y_i}\sigma_i v_p(f_i(x))}\,dx.
\]
For each $t\in[1,2]$, let $\boldsymbol\sigma(t)$ be the unique saddle in the admissible
region satisfying
\[
 \frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma_i}
 \left(\sum_{p\le y_i}\log Z_{p,\mathbf y}(\boldsymbol\sigma)
       +\sum_j\sigma_j\log|f_j(Nt)|\right)=0
 \qquad(1\le i\le s).
\]
Uniformly when the smoothness parameters $u_i(t)=\log|f_i(Nt)|/\log y_i$ remain in a
fixed compact saddle-point range, the joint smooth-value probability is asymptotic to
the integral over $t\in[1,2]$ of the product of the marginal saddle densities multiplied
by
\[
 \mathcal E(\boldsymbol\sigma(t))=
 \prod_p\frac{Z_{p,\mathbf y}(\boldsymbol\sigma(t))}
 {\prod_i Z_{p,i,y_i}(\sigma_i(t))}.
\]
Here $Z_{p,i,y_i}$ is the one-polynomial partition function obtained from
$Z_{p,\mathbf y}$ by retaining the factor $f_i$ alone, and the marginal saddle density
of $f_i$ at $t$ is the one-polynomial saddle-point expression
$\exp\bigl(\sum_{p\le y_i}\log Z_{p,i,y_i}(\sigma^{*}_i(t))
+\sigma^{*}_i(t)\log|f_i(Nt)|\bigr)$ at the one-polynomial saddle
$\sigma^{*}_i(t)$, with the usual Gaussian curvature factor.  After both this archimedean
integration and the moving $p$-adic Euler factor are included,
the remaining relative error tends to zero.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight.}
The conjecture is adelic rather than purely Eulerian.  It includes exact higher
valuations, the variation of polynomial sizes across a dyadic interval, and the movement
of the local law under the smoothness tilt, so no assertion that
all global correlation is $p$-adic is smuggled in.  Single-polynomial smooth-value
estimates are already
difficult \cite{MartinSmooth,Mine}, and a multivariate adelic saddle law would be a
major new
local--global bridge.

\begin{conjecture}[Buchstab--Bateman--Horn factor-count flow]\label{c25}
Let $f\in\Z[x]$ be primitive, irreducible, and admissible, and sample
$n\in[N,2N]$.  For $y$ with $u_n=\log|f(n)|/\log y$ in a fixed compact subset of
$(1,\infty)$, let $\Omega_y(f(n))$ be the number of prime factors of $f(n)$ exceeding
$y$, counted with multiplicity, on the event $P^-(f(n))>y$.  Write $\rho_f(p)$ for
the number of roots of $f$ modulo $p$, $V_f(y)=\prod_{p\le y}(1-\rho_f(p)/p)$ for
the sieve density, and $C_f$ for the Bateman--Horn constant of $f$.

Define universal Buchstab component functions by
\[
 \omega_1(u)=\frac1u,
 \qquad
 \omega_j(u)=\frac{1}{u(j-1)!}
 \int_{\substack{t_1,\dots,t_{j-1}\ge1\\t_1+\cdots+t_{j-1}\le u-1}}
 \frac{dt_1\cdots dt_{j-1}}{t_1\cdots t_{j-1}}
 \quad(j\ge2),
\]
with an empty integral interpreted as zero.  These satisfy
$\sum_{j\ge1}\omega_j(u)=\omega(u)$.  Then for every fixed $j$,
\[
 \Pp\bigl(P^-(f(n))>y,\ \Omega_y(f(n))=j\bigr)
 =V_f(y)e^\gamma\,\E_{n\in[N,2N]}\omega_j(u_n)+o(V_f(y)),
\]
uniformly on compact $u$-ranges.  Summing over $j$ gives the rough-value Buchstab law.
The $j=1$ component and $V_f(y)\sim C_fe^{-\gamma}/\log y$ give the Bateman--Horn
prime-value density, while $j=2$ gives the semiprime transition.  If
$\max_{N\le n\le2N}|f(n)|<y^2$, every rough value belongs to the $j=1$ component
exactly.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Insight and diagnostic.}
Primes, semiprimes, and all higher almost-prime transitions are now parts of one process,
not a conditional probability obtained by dividing two assumed asymptotics.  The
polynomial local factors enter only through $V_f$, while the remaining-factor count is a
universal finite-$u$ flow.  This is strongly falsifiable: even if total roughness follows
Buchstab, the distribution among $j$ can fail.  The script compares the observed
component vector for $n^2+1$ with both the displayed universal profile and a matched
random-integer sample, and checks the pointwise endpoint.

\section{Quality audit and research priorities}

The collection is not flat.  The strongest parent mechanisms are
\cref{c1,c3,c4,c6,c10,c11,c12,c13,c15,c16,c17,c21,c23,c24,c25}.  The remaining conjectures are
structural consequences or extensions that introduce a distinct invariant: overlap
rank, Gauss-transfer duality, hypertree corrections, terminal occupancy, exceptional spectral
response, rank-depth processes, dynatomic colouring, growing-degree squarefreeness,
divisor-sensitive gcd geometry and residual-mass decoupling.

The first decisive targets are deliberately smaller than the full statements:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove the third-cumulant diagram formula for two pair motifs and identify the
connected disjoint kernel.
\item Derive $A_2(t)$ in \cref{c6} and show whether $A_3(t)$ is genuinely one
logarithmic order smaller.
\item Prove a rank-one version of the large-sieve inequality \cref{c12} for
$M=X^\delta$.
\item Prove bounded-level stability of the arboreal sampling functional under one explicit open subgroup and compare the resulting critical depths.
\item Prove a two-colour restricted-support version of \cref{c21} or \cref{c23}.
\item Prove the $j=1$ and $j=2$ component laws of \cref{c25} in an averaged polynomial family before attempting the full fixed-polynomial flow.
\end{enumerate}

A conjecture should be removed rather than defended if its canonical object fails.  The
collection accordingly contains no claim that a finite computation certifies novelty,
no probability on a deterministic sequence without an ensemble, no positive-density
Siegel-zero family, no functorial toric logarithm based on incompatible group exponents,
and no scalar Euler factor pretending to specify a full point-process interaction.
